### Saint Paul After Dark traces the city’s protection arrangement in a seven-chapter digital tour

**SAINT PAUL, Minn. · [RELEASE DATE]**

Saint Paul After Dark is an independent digital history tour about the informal protection arrangement associated with the city’s underworld, the people who made it work, and those who bore its costs. The tour spans 1900 to 1938 and can be explored from home.

The seven chapters take readers through locations associated with the arrangement and the events that strained it. “Under the Bluff” begins with a 1933 business notice for Castle Royal and looks at how later recollections shaped the nightclub’s reputation. Readers can follow each chapter into the sources, where claims carry explanations and qualifications.

The tour includes a map and directory of places, a casebook of people and connections, and optional synthesized narration for chapter introductions. Each chapter can also be read on screen. Artwork is identified as illustration.

Enter the story at [TOUR URL]. Contact [CONTACT] for questions and approved images.

## Story pitches

- **Local history feature.** How did a city’s informal arrangement move risk from one set of people to another? Start with the prologue and let the epilogue’s question, “Who absorbed the consequences?”, guide the story.
- **One object, two kinds of evidence.** The empty chair in “Under the Bluff” introduces the distance between a 1933 business notice and later stories about its patrons. Offer the chapter link, illustration credit, and links to the underlying sources.
- **Public history and teaching.** Follow a paragraph to its claim, qualification, and source. The method page and downloadable citations show readers how to inspect and discuss the case. Offer this as an optional resource to librarians and educators.

## Questions from editors

**Can readers take the tour at home?**  
Yes. It is a browser-based story with seven chapters, a map and directory, and linked research. Readers can start with the story or choose a chapter.

**What does the map show?**  
It places some venues approximately against the modern Mississippi River and lists all seven places in the directory. The venue is the source for any current access information.

**What will readers hear?**  
Chapter introductions have optional synthesized narration. Some scenes offer separate synthesized atmosphere, which readers can choose to play. The text of each introduction is available on its chapter page.

**How can a reader check a claim?**  
Use a chapter’s evidence links or the claim ledger under Sources. Each ledger entry gives the source, a passage locator, and the limits of the claim. The ledger also gives a route for suggesting a correction.

**What is the status of the edition?**  
Use the status on the current edition notes. Edition 0.5.0 is currently marked as a working preview with human reviews pending. If a later edition is the subject, cite its actual revision and review record.

**How should an editor credit the images and cite the project?**  
Use the credit and permission supplied for that file. Cite the tour name, content edition, page address, and date accessed. Add a claim ID when reporting on a specific assertion. The [Sources template](../src/pages/sources.astro) holds the project’s detailed citation format.

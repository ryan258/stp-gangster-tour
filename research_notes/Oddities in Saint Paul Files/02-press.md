# Newspaper dispatches

## What stopped Roger Touhy’s car near Elkhorn?

- The September 1 *Kewaskum Statesman* reports that a car carrying Roger Touhy, Eddie McFadden, Gus Schaefer and Willie Sharkey skidded into a telephone pole near Elkhorn, Wisconsin. It names Harry Ward as the unarmed officer and reports the four men’s transfer to Saint Paul in the William Hamm Jr. case. The Illinois Supreme Court opinion in the separate John Factor case dates the crash and arrest to July 19, 1933 and discusses guns seized from the car. [*Kewaskum Statesman*, “Wisconsin News,” Milwaukee item](https://kewaskumhistoricalsociety.org/assets/newspapers/1933/kts-1933-09-01.pdf), [*People v. Touhy*, 361 Ill. 332, paragraph beginning “Touhy, Stevens and McFadden filed a motion to suppress”](https://syfert.com/illinois/cases/361-ill-332/the-people-v-touhy)
- Touhy recalled that an officer named $22.50 as the replacement cost and that he agreed to pay it. The county’s account has Ward bring the men in about the damages. It says Sharkey subsequently rode a chicken truck to Lake Geneva and called the jail about the pole, after which a deputy arrested him. The bill amount and the truck story each come from a later account. [Roger Touhy with Ray Brennan, *The Stolen Years*, chapter 10, “Gone Fishing,” 1959](https://rogertouhygangsters.blogspot.com/2013/04/the-stolen-years-by-roger-touhy-in.html), [Walworth County Sheriff’s Office, “Roger Touhy” and “Arrest & Parole”](https://www.co.walworth.wi.us/252/History)
- The *Manchester Evening Herald* ran a front-page photograph showing Elkhorn deputies holding Touhy and described it as his first newspaper or police photograph. Its caption records the Wisconsin crash and the contemporaneous Factor inquiry. The November AP verdict report records acquittal for the four men on the federal Hamm counts. [*Manchester Evening Herald*, July 22, 1933, p. 1, “First Photo of Roger Touhy, Chicago Gangster”](https://cdn.manchesterhistory.org/News/Manchester%20Evening%20Hearld_1933-07-22.pdf), [*Medford Mail Tribune*, November 28, 1933, p. 1, “Fail to Convict Touhy Gangsters”](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn97071090/1933-11-28/ed-1/seq-1/ocr/)

The existing Casebook stories begin with the later transfer, hearings and Saint Paul trial. The comparison follows the Wisconsin crash and separates contemporary records from the memoir and county account. The precise bill remains unverified, as does the photograph’s claimed priority. The county history gives an unreliable parole date. The Wisconsin newspaper’s weapon inventory and the guns contested in the Illinois opinion also differ, so the draft leaves quantities out.

## Why did a prosecutor say mail thieves needed two kinds of keys?

- A May 9, 1936 United Press dispatch reports U.S. Attorney Thomas J. Morrissey’s account of an alleged conspiracy. He said that Jack Segar of Saint Paul obtained railroad switch keys and keys that fit post office mailbags in Denver in 1931. The continuation says Segar returned to Saint Paul and recruited a crew including John Edwards and a man the dispatch identifies as Winston W. Clement, alias Walker. [*Sapulpa Herald*, “Bare Conspiracy To Rob U.S. Mail And Peddle Loot,” May 9, 1936, p. 1](https://gateway.okhistory.org/ark:/67531/metadc1524843/m1/1/), [p. 5 continuation](https://gateway.okhistory.org/ark:/67531/metadc1524843/m1/5/)
- Morrissey said the crew targeted small railway stations where mail waited overnight, entered the mailroom with the switch keys, opened sacks with the other set, and took registered mail. He said they relocked both the sacks and the building. [*Sapulpa Herald*, May 9, 1936, p. 5, paragraphs beginning “In many small towns”](https://gateway.okhistory.org/ark:/67531/metadc1524843/m1/5/)
- The prosecutor said a $100 Liberty bond passed by a Denver relief worker had led investigators to more than two dozen mail thefts. The paper reported eleven arrests and named Segar and Clement among those held in Saint Paul. [*Sapulpa Herald*, May 9, 1936, p. 1, May 9 Denver dateline, arrest list and paragraph on the bond](https://gateway.okhistory.org/ark:/67531/metadc1524843/m1/1/)

This account involves different men and a different operation from the Casebook’s Garrettsville train holdup and stolen bond found in a food can. The page 1 and page 5 links are two parts of the same wire story. A same-day Ames edition also carried an account of the arrests. The prosecutor’s claims about the crew and the keys remain attributed. A case disposition and a list of the stations remain unverified. [*Ames Daily Tribune and Times*, “Mail Robbery Plot Uncovered In Denver Case,” May 9, 1936, p. 1, archive summary](https://newspaperarchive.com/ames-daily-tribune-and-times-may-09-1936-p-1/)

# Whiskey under scrap iron

- [Goldberg v. United States, 277 F. 211](https://openjurist.org/277/f1d/211/goldberg-v-united-states), pp. 213–215, reproduces the indictment, with a car of 15 barrels on January 2, PMCKY 40479 with 25 on January 7, and Penn. 281781 with 24 on January 16. The cars came from Winnipeg with the whiskey allegedly buried in scrap iron. The court explains that the import ban predated national Prohibition. It affirmed the judgment. The figures are counts in the indictment, and the browser labels them as such. Avoid giving a combined shipment total as fact because the later MNopedia article gives 74 while the three counts in the quoted indictment add to 64.
- [In re Mason, 147 Minn. 383](https://law.counselstack.com/opinion/in-re-mason-minn-1920), p. 394, is Quinn’s dissent describing a bank vault entry on December 26, the first car reaching Saint Paul on December 29 and Minneapolis on December 31, and unloading on January 1 or 2. He used the dates to dispute Weisman’s testimony about paying Nash after unloading. The appellate transcript supplies the dates and Quinn’s reading of them.
- The state opinion, pp. 385, 387–392, records Nash’s federal acquittal, the governor’s removal and the limited review that left it in place. [Paul Nelson’s MNopedia account](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/winnipeg-liquor-conspiracy) places Burnquist’s hearing at the State Capitol and names the original file, no. 22065. The rail shipments and the court opinion make this a distinct 1919–1920 ring from the Gleeman brothers’ later railroad-car case. Saint Paul is both a reported rail waypoint and the location of the removal hearing.

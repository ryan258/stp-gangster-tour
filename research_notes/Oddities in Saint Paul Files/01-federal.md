# A wedding arrest and a clothing-store warning

## The wedding and the two calls

- A Bureau memorandum in press-release form reports that Bruno Austin, also known as Bruno Putis, was taken into custody at the end of his wedding ceremony in Chicago on January 20, 1935. It lists Austin among the defendants when the Bremer trial opened in Saint Paul on April 15. The Bureau’s later summary identifies Austin as the Whitey named in the January 22 conspiracy indictment. The wedding memo does not describe the venue or guests. [April 15 memorandum, pp. 1–2, file 7-576-5572X](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20123/at_download/file) · [Bremer investigation summary, pp. 44–45](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/at_download/file)
- Special Agent M. Shafetz reported that Mrs. Austin came to the Chicago Bureau office on March 18. She said her mother answered two calls on March 11 from people who claimed to be federal men and said they wanted to question her. Shafetz said the Bureau had made neither call. The account describes the two calls together and supplies no individual dialogue, caller identity or motive. The same report records Mrs. Austin saying that they were secretly married after meeting in summer 1934. It gives no date for the private marriage and does not explain its relationship to the January ceremony. [March 18 interview, printed p. 88](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20112/at_download/file)
- The Bureau summary says the Saint Paul court discharged Austin from custody May 6. It separately records his rearrest in Chicago, October 11 murder conviction and life sentence there. The Court’s release order and the October judgment are described in the summary. [Bremer investigation summary, p. 45, paragraph beginning ‘Bruno Austin on May 6, 1935’](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/at_download/file)

The call log pairs two call entries with Shafetz’s later office response. The entries share the surviving account of the two calls. The wedding, interview and custody outcome are separate from the Casebook’s earlier Bremer money-changing and courtroom stories.

## The manhunt at the clothing counter

- The Bureau’s investigation summary links police locating a Buick in a garage to the visit to the Atlantic City hotel on January 20, 1935. It reports that a man in underwear offered to fetch the person the officers wanted, then describes Karpis reaching Campbell’s room and the two men escaping after exchanging gunfire. The paragraph gives no name for the man in the hallway. The Saint Paul indictments section identifies both fugitives in the Bremer case. [Bremer investigation summary, pp. 41 and 44](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/at_download/file)
- An Associated Press dispatch says police asked clothing stores to watch for men trying to buy clothes. It lists Karpis in slippers, trousers and an overcoat, and Campbell in underwear, slippers and an overcoat. Authorities believed the men were still in Atlantic City with three highway bridges guarded. Another AP report compresses Campbell’s outfit to underwear alone. The dispatch story attributes its descriptions to the Big Spring item. [The Big Spring Daily Herald, January 21, 1935, front page, Atlantic City dateline](https://newspapers.swco.ttu.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/46d5eb78-5306-4ddf-a2c4-184ad817d6b3/content) · [Pampa Daily News, January 21, 1935, John W. Lear dispatch](https://newspapers.swco.ttu.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/d94ed183-5b5e-4a61-b296-97307787ca05/content)
- The Bureau’s later summary places both men in a stolen Pontiac after they left the hotel. It traces the car to Quakertown and the men to the abduction of Dr. Horace Hunsicker near Sellersville, Pennsylvania. It supplies no exact exit route or clothing-store visit. The wire establishes what the authorities believed when the alert went out. It gives no reliable chronology for the placement of bridge guards relative to the fugitives leaving. [Bremer investigation summary, p. 41, last Atlantic City paragraph](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/at_download/file)

The dispatch separates the search as police described it from the route in the later investigation. The Casebook’s earlier Karpis capture, forced landing and post–Lake Weir leads cover other events.

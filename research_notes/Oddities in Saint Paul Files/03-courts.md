# The two Colts with aluminum grips

## What did the court record connect to the Como Park getaway?

### Takeaway

The Minnesota Supreme Court reviewed eyewitness testimony from the getaway, two pistols with similar alterations, and a Wabasha Street haircut alibi that conflicted with Hankins’s own account.

### Cited findings

- After the December 16, 1932 Third Northwestern National Bank robbery in Minneapolis, police found the robbers’ disabled Lincoln in Saint Paul’s Como Park. J. H. Peterson testified that he passed the men moving a white sack into a smaller car and identified Leonard Hankins, alias Owen Lewis, as the man carrying it. The court records the deaths of Minneapolis officers Ira L. Evans and Leo Gorski and Hankins’s first-degree murder conviction. [*State v. Hankins, Alias Lewis*, 193 Minn. 375, 258 N.W. 578, printed pp. 375–377, ¶¶1–5](https://openjurist.org/193/minn/375/state-v-hankins-alias-lewis)
- De Vol was arrested at a Saint Paul apartment. Hankins was arrested separately in Minneapolis. The opinion describes two .45-caliber Colt pistols with unusual aluminum grips, factory numbers chiseled away, and a prosecution expert who said the same tool made the marks. The extended magazine was recovered with De Vol. Hankins’s pistol was exhibit K. [*Hankins*, printed p. 377, ¶¶6–7 and p. 379, ¶11](https://openjurist.org/193/minn/375/state-v-hankins-alias-lewis)
- Hankins said Springer gave him the weapon the evening before the arrest. The state called witnesses who described his prior possession of a similar weapon. A Saint Paul barber said he was cutting Hankins’s hair on Wabasha Street at the relevant time. Hankins’s own statement to authorities put him traveling from Superior that afternoon and arriving in Saint Paul about five. The court affirmed the order denying a new trial and said this opinion superseded an earlier one. [*Hankins*, printed pp. 379–382, ¶¶11–14, 19–20](https://openjurist.org/193/minn/375/state-v-hankins-alias-lewis)

### Gaps

- The public opinion summarizes testimony. It gives no photo of either pistol, identity of the person who altered them, or provenance of the specific tool. It reports Peterson’s identification without establishing the white sack’s contents.

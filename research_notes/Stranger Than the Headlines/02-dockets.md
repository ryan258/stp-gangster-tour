# The lamps at police headquarters

## What did Wallace Jamie put inside the public safety building?

### Takeaway

Contemporary coverage of Saint Paul’s police corruption inquiry describes concealed microphones, a device adapted from fire alarm recorders, and a recorder discovered in a ventilation shaft.

### Cited findings

- The August 16, 1935 *Vernonia Eagle*, p. 2, describes Jamie asking to observe police at work and placing microphones in lamps and telephones. The July 1936 illustrated feature says he posed as an efficiency expert and describes microphones in lamps and beneath chairs and desks. [“He Stirred St. Paul”](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn99063854/1935-08-16/ed-1/seq-2/), [“Young Detective Smashes Police Ring,” printed p. 53 and continuation p. 127](https://electronicsandbooks.com/edt/manual/Old%20Pulp%20Magazines/%21%20new/Modern%20Mechanix.%20-%20July%201936.pdf)
- The magazine says shorthand transcription proved slow, a pamograph cut conversations into aluminum discs with a sapphire needle, and a fire alarm recorder adapted for telephone lines marked dialed digits on tape. Its Duncan number is explicitly hypothetical. The same account says a janitor found one of Jamie’s dictaphones in a ventilation shaft and recordings reached the mayor and city and county attorneys. [Modern Mechanix, printed p. 127, “Ticker Tape Records Telephone Numbers” and “Janitor Tips Off Jamie’s Plot”](https://electronicsandbooks.com/edt/manual/Old%20Pulp%20Magazines/%21%20new/Modern%20Mechanix.%20-%20July%201936.pdf)
- Cowie dates Jamie’s arrival to May 1934 and links his work to a 1935 Ramsey County grand jury inquiry and twenty-one indictments. The magazine places him at the station in late winter or early spring 1935. MNHS also documents the bugging of police headquarters and the 1935 discipline of officials. [Ramsey County History, printed p. 13](https://rchs.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/RCHS_Fall2000_Cowie.pdf#page=12), [MNHS chronology, July 24, 1935](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/event/st-paul-police-department-reform-1933-1940)

### Inferences

- An evidence interaction can open the reported hiding places, compare the audio recorder with the digit ticker, and follow the magazine’s account of the discovery into the county record. The Casebook’s Raasch story covers courtroom playback and the Cedar 5328 line. This story uses the contemporary coverage of Jamie’s investigation, physical placement of equipment, and what the ticker captured.

### Gaps

- The exact installation dates, equipment layouts, original ticker strips and disc contents were unavailable in these sources. The magazine alone supplies the janitor episode and the adapted fire alarm recorder. The two accounts use different descriptions of Jamie’s cover. An invented sample dial may use the magazine’s explicitly hypothetical example if clearly labeled, or the interaction can omit the digits.

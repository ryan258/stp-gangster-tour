# Newspaper dispatches

## Who identified the Phantom Gunman in Saint Paul?

### Cited findings

- The Associated Press reported that California drug company cashier David Antink identified Morrie Moll in Saint Paul in spring 1929 as a participant in the 1923 robbery. The same item links the name Morrie Miller and the nickname Phantom Gunman to Moll. After Antink was killed in Hollywood in September 1929, Los Angeles District Attorney Buron Fitts accused Saint Paul authorities and Los Angeles politicians of blocking the transfer, and police sought Moll as a suspect. [The Eugene Guard, September 27, 1929, p. 1, Los Angeles dateline, paragraphs beginning with Fitts’s statement and the police search](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn96088108/1929-09-27/ed-1/seq-1/)
- The United Press dispatch gives the reported $10,000 bail and, under “Extradition Tried,” attributes to Fitts the claim that Minnesota Governor Theodore Christianson refused a warrant. [Albany Democrat-Herald, “Killed in Revenge for Foiling Crime,” September 27, 1929, p. 1](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn96088460/1929-09-27/ed-1/seq-1.pdf)
- J. H. Graham’s later account places the identification on June 5, 1929 in the governor’s office. It says extradition was granted on June 15 and that Moll disappeared after release on $10,000 bond. The two accounts agree that the transfer failed. [J. H. Graham, November 10, 2016, paragraphs immediately below the “Moll arrested in Florida 2-6-1929” caption](https://jhgraham.com/2016/11/10/the-pajama-kid-the-phantom-gunman-and-a-shooting-at-the-bella-napoli/)
- The AP still reported Antink’s killing unsolved in 1933. [Pampa Daily News, August 29, 1933, Hollywood dateline, report on a restaurant shooting and paragraphs on Harry Mackley, David Antink and Morris Moll](https://newspapers.swco.ttu.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/a1bbcd8e-5a04-4762-8463-847f0debbd52/content)

### Overlap and limits

The Casebook already covers the Gleeman brothers’ unsuccessful appeal and their account of the Twin Cities liquor syndicate. The 1929 encounter and interstate warrant dispute took place two years later. The Minnesota Supreme Court opinion records the Gleemans’ allegations about Morrie Miller, and the 1929 papers identify him as Moll. The June 5 date and office are from the later account. The underlying extradition papers and June newspaper coverage remain unverified. The available dispatches give no finding about who killed Antink. [State v. Gleeman, 170 Minn. 197, 200–204](https://openjurist.org/170/minn/197/state-v-gleeman)

## How did a story about an alligator enter the Bremer investigation?

### Cited findings

- The expected testimony outline for Byron Bolton places him and William Harrison at a pecan ranch roughly 30 to 50 miles south of Macon, Georgia, on their return from Florida. Bolton said he overheard Russell Gibson and Doc Barker discuss a lake, a cottage, a motorboat, deer and a local alligator called Big Joe. The next page identifies the original question-and-answer record taken in the United States Attorney’s Saint Paul office. [FBI Vault, Bremer Kidnapping Part 122, Byron Bolton outline, printed pp. 110–111](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20122)
- The bureau’s February memorandum reports finding two Florida maps in Doc Barker’s Chicago apartment, with marks at Ocala and Lake Weir. On January 11, Bolton said he thought the marks indicated the hideout, which he associated with Highway 41 and Big Joe. Agents investigated the Ocala area January 13–15 and located the cottage and its occupants, Fred and Kate Barker. [FBI Vault, Bremer Kidnapping Part 108, February 8, 1935 memorandum for the director, pp. 1–2, file mark 7-576-5072](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20108)
- The 1936 investigation summary mentions a marked map and an alligator named Old Joe. It describes the search that located the Barkers at the Lake Weir cottage. [FBI Vault, Bremer Investigation Summary, November 19, 1936, printed pp. 38–39](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/at_download/file)

### Overlap and limits

The existing Casebook entries cover separate parts of the Saint Paul Bremer kidnapping and investigation. Here the map follows the reported conversation near Macon, the Chicago maps and the Florida inquiries. The ranch location is approximate. The bureau’s sources use two nicknames for the alligator and describe the maps differently. Bolton’s interpretation of the map marks preceded the Florida inquiries that established the cottage’s location and its occupants. The records give several leads and leave the alligator’s precise contribution open.

# The letters about Billie Frechette’s act

- The [FBI’s Dillinger case history](https://www.fbi.gov/history/cases-and-criminals/john-dillinger), S11, places Frechette’s conspiracy trial and conviction in Saint Paul after the Lincoln Court shootout.
- [The Billboard, July 11, 1936, p. 51](https://www.worldradiohistory.com/Archive-All-Music/Billboard/30s/1936/Billboard-1936-07-11.pdf) names Frechette in Scout Younger’s Arizona Dust Bowl. The Johnstown dispatch calls her prison a state prison. The [June 18 Associated Press report, Daily Iowan p. 2](https://dailyiowan.lib.uiowa.edu/DI/1936/di1936-06-18.pdf) identifies it as a federal prison in Milan, Michigan. Use the trade paper for the booking and the FBI history for the sentence.
- [The Billboard, January 9, 1937, p. 50](https://device.report/m/49a9758fa9dfedba4c0114ba21e5fb727c8b243043925c8ae6e1409148e09f44.pdf) names Frechette as the featured performer in Crime Does Not Pay and describes a photograph with the booking agent, sound advertising man, emcee and several unnamed characters in wax. The file is a scan of the printed page. An auction listing describes a 1936 press photo as featuring a wax Dillinger. The Casebook keeps the figures unnamed because the trade report does.
- The [Frechette FBI file](https://vault.fbi.gov/Evelyn%20Frechette%20/Evelyn%20Frechette%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29) has the April 8 letter from Frank H. Heath, who saw Haigler Theater posters in Camden, South Carolina, and suspected impersonation. The May 29 Pittsburgh letter gives the Bureau stenographer’s identification of Frechette from photographs and paraphrases her answers. The Pittsburgh identification leaves the Camden booking open. The two letters bracket the show’s claim to an appearance “in person.”
- The browser playbill follows the booking, cast list, letter and audience exchange. Treat the cast and Q&A as sourced reading cards. No fabricated playbill, photo, audio or verbatim questions. The Casebook’s related Lincoln Court stories cover the shootout, tenants, possessions and other details before the trial. This follows Frechette into public performance after the sentence.

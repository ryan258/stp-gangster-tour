# An armored Hudson and a bond in a food can

## The Hudson that made its own smoke

- An Associated Press dispatch quotes Dakota County Attorney Harold Stassen’s findings and recounts the August 30, 1933 payroll robbery outside the South Saint Paul post office. It describes smoke coming from the car, a siren and the bank messengers taking cover under a truck. The robbery killed Officer Leo Pavlak and wounded Officer John Yeaman. The story links the sedan to a September Chicago mail robbery. [Fairmont Daily Sentinel, March 12, 1935, “Machine Gun Used Stolen From Police” and “Auto Also Identified,” FBI Vault Part 108](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20108)
- A 1935 federal report describes a black 1933 Hudson abandoned after a collision following the Chicago mail robbery. Its inventory lists a siren, smoke-screen apparatus, bulletproof glass beside the driver and steel plate around the sides. The report traces the modifications to a Chicago body shop and gives neither the exact placement of the smoke and siren hardware nor their operating details. The interactive diagram uses these four recorded components. [D. P. Sullivan’s July 29, 1935 report, printed pp. 9–12, FBI Vault Part 149](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20149/at_download/file)
- The same report says the abandoned car bore Florida plates 112-185 registered to a Chevrolet sedan. Among other sets inside was Minnesota pair B141186, issued to a Duluth Ford. Sullivan says Chicago police linked that pair to the August South Saint Paul robbery. The press inventory gives a longer list of states than Sullivan’s list, so the story uses the two specific registrations established by his report. [Sullivan report, printed pp. 11–12, plate inventory and checks](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20149/at_download/file)

The existing Cleveland notebook story uses the South Saint Paul robbery as context for a route found in Ohio. This reconstruction follows the car and the plates. Dillinger’s separate Hudson trade-in appears elsewhere in the Casebook.

## The bond in the food can

- The Bureau’s 1936 case summary places Jess Doyle, Fred and Arthur Barker, and Alvin Karpis at the Twin Oaks apartments in Saint Paul in late 1932. The federal conspiracy indictment in the Bremer case named Doyle in January 1935. The charges against him were dismissed May 6. The Kansas City office recorded Doyle’s companion saying they used the name Stacey at 23 Warner Plaza in Kansas City, Missouri, where the report placed him at the time of the indictment. [Bremer investigation summary, Twin Oaks and printed pp. 44–45](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/at_download/file) · [Kansas City follow-up, p. 1, FBI Vault Part 128](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20128/)
- A Kansas City telegram records that federal agents searched the apartment and skipped food containers in the refrigerator. It relays the local press report of a janitor finding a stolen bond in a can, reports the bond was turned over to local police and says verification would follow. The can’s contents are unclear in the reproduced text. The search scene distinguishes the Bureau’s description of its own search from the report about the janitor. [Western Union Kansas City telegram KA17 72, signed Nathan, March 20, 1935 stamp, FBI Vault Part 108](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20108)
- A September 1935 letter confirms the apartment recovery and gives the $1,000 City of Peabody bond’s serial number as 56. It relays Volney Davis’s competing account that the bond came from an Oklahoma mail robbery. Agent R. P. Shanahan’s February 21 report records a Bank of Drexel cashier identifying it as one taken on December 13, 1934. Conroy also says Shanahan reported Doyle’s admission that he, Davis and John Langdon robbed the bank. The letter uses a different street spelling and leaves the container and finder to the earlier report. [E. L. Conroy’s Kansas City letter to Oklahoma City, handwritten p. 67, FBI Vault Part 157](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20157/at_download/file)

The bond was property stolen in a bank robbery. The Bremer file is where the Bureau preserved the search and correspondence. The earlier cornmeal-box story follows a ransom note left at a Faribault hotel. The portable-refrigerator story follows a retail transaction with a different person and object.

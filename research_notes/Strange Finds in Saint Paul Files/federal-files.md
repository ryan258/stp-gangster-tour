# Federal case files

## What did the investigators find after the Lincoln Court escape?

### Takeaway

The Saint Paul Division of Investigation inventoried family photographs and traced a road chart found at Lincoln Court. Apartment manager Daisy Coffey told agents she found a partial telegram beneath the carpet. The records document the artifacts and their limits.

### Cited findings

- The inventory records a photograph of a boy sitting in a cherry tree. It quotes a pencil note on the back identifying the boy as John Dillinger at ten or eleven. Agents also recorded a small boy in a wagon and a photograph of Dillinger in naval uniform. [Saint Paul file 26-2434, section 10, after the printed p. 11 marker](https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/fbifiles/gangsterera/johndillinger/dillinger%20gang%20section%2010.pdf)
- The Kansas City field office reported that agent O. C. Dewey traced a road chart from Newton National Bank in Newton, Iowa, to Saint Paul. Investigators suspected a bank getaway route and sent the original chart to the laboratory. A later Treasury history says law enforcement waited at the bank on April 4, 1934, and the expected robbers did not arrive. [Saint Paul file 26-2434, section 10, between printed p. 10 and 11 markers](https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/fbifiles/gangsterera/johndillinger/dillinger%20gang%20section%2010.pdf), [Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, Newton National Bank history](https://occ.treas.gov/about/who-we-are/history/history-of-the-occ/1914-1935/1914-1935-newton-national-bank.html)
- The inventory report records that after investigators removed the articles, Mrs. Coffey gave Special Agent in Charge Hanni a portion of a Western Union telegram she reported finding under the carpet. The report transcribes the fragment as “Received three will appreciate your help must act at once.” [Saint Paul file 26-2434, section 10, two paragraphs before the printed p. 16 marker](https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/fbifiles/gangsterera/johndillinger/dillinger%20gang%20section%2010.pdf), [FBI Vault’s listing of the same archival section as Part 11](https://vault.fbi.gov/John%20Dillinger%20/john/John%20Dillinger%20Gang%20Part%2011/view)

### Inferences

- The source fits an evidence table where readers turn over the photographs, trace the chart endpoints, then reveal Coffey’s telegram fragment. The quoted caption is recorded text and the displayed items are descriptions from the inventory.

### Gaps

- The inventory gives no author for the caption and no sender, recipient, or meaning for the telegram fragment. It does not reproduce the map or prove who drew it or used it. The later Treasury account adds detail about Newton but does not reproduce the original chart.

## Who came back for the dishes on Grand Avenue?

### Takeaway

The Barker–Karpis investigation preserves a caretaker’s account of an apartment move, a penciled pickup note for silverware, and a utility inquiry into three small deposits. The material ties these mundane clues to an investigation of a Saint Paul gang hideout and leaves some identifications unsettled.

### Cited findings

- Mr. Parnell reported seeing black suitcases carried to cars after returning with his wife from a basketball game in early March 1933. He called police, then watched from a laundry room. A door squeaked and a man turned with a revolver. Parnell later thought a photograph of Arthur Barker resembled the man. [Barker Karpis Part 77, archive stamp 323 and following passage](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/barker-karpis/Barker%20Karpis%20Part%2077), [Bremer Part 171, review citing O. G. Hall’s March 7, 1934 investigation, printed pp. 4–5](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20171/)
- The Parnell account says police removed clothing and bedding, leaving silverware and dishes. A mover called for the remaining property on April 26, 1933 and presented a pencil note addressed to Coy Hardwick. One report says Mr. Parnell noted the Ford truck’s plate number and the investigation followed it through motor vehicle and dealer records. A February 26, 1934 letter says the office sent the note and a separate letter for handwriting and typewriting comparison in the Bremer case. [Barker Karpis Part 77, passage leading up to archive stamp 330](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/barker-karpis/Barker%20Karpis%20Part%2077), [Bremer Part 17, February 26 letter, printed p. 2 after archive-stamped p. 52, paragraph beginning “The letter addressed to Mr.Coy O.Hardwick”](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%2017)
- A separate report records a $6 gas and electricity deposit and two similar deposits. After service ended, two refund checks were returned unclaimed. The third was not returned and the utility showed no credit remaining. The office retained signature tracings from the utility applications. [Bremer Part 29, PDF p. 13, Saint Paul file 7-30, report stamped 7-576-1384](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%2029/at_download/file#page=13)
- A contemporaneous police report from March 4, 1933 lists an abandoned steamer trunk and grip. The later FBI file says the collected clothes and other articles did not identify the occupants. It records Inspector Charles Tierney’s suspicion of a tip-off and his inability to locate the other responding officers’ report. [Barker Karpis Part 77, Talbott and Tierney interviews following vehicle list](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/barker-karpis/Barker%20Karpis%20Part%2077)

### Inferences

- The papers offer three reader actions in order. Follow the caretaker’s vantage, open the mover’s pickup note, and check what happened to the deposits. The inspector’s suspicion of a tip-off belongs to his account.

### Gaps

- The reports differ on which Parnell released the dishes and on apartment numbers and initials. The note’s signers’ identities, delivery destination, scientific comparison result and the third check’s recipient remain unresolved. The Parnells could not identify the occupants with certainty.

## Reserve. What happened to the ransom typewriter leads?

### Takeaway

The Bremer inquiry generated sample lines to type, a search of about 500 repair slips and an internal memo about evidence left in a federal supply room. It is a strong follow-up reserve.

### Cited findings

- The February 26, 1934 Saint Paul memo recommends typing three test phrases on paper similar to the ransom notes, including the familiar quick brown fox sentence. It says the laboratory was looking for a 1929 portable Corona with pica type and discusses a local machine whose former ribbon was purple and slightly smudgy. The memo concludes that this machine did not appear to be the one sought. [Bremer Part 17, February 26 memorandum, archive-stamped pp. 107–108](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%2017)
- Agent J. E. Brennan’s memorandum records the search through roughly 500 slips in eight months of L. C. Smith & Corona repair records. Two entries fit the type. One was a machine sent in from a Saint Paul boiler and machinery company and returned after a $1.50 repair. The inquiry described the typewriter as having a three-row keyboard. [Barker Karpis Part 77, Brennan’s “Re: Typewriters” memorandum, before archive stamp 320](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/barker-karpis/Barker%20Karpis%20Part%2077)
- A September 27, 1934 memo records that an Underwood recovered from the Cleveland apartment occupied by Karpis and others was initially held as Bureau property. A clerk found it on a supply-room shelf in a case with a painted room number. An October 2 letter says the shipment lacked the laboratory labels. The September memo says samples could not be obtained, suggests checking for latent prints, and gives the lab’s tentative identification for the Hamm and Bremer notes as a portable Corona. [Bremer Part 59, R. E. Newby’s September 27 memo and October 2 follow-up, archive-stamped pp. 239–241](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%2059/)

### Inferences

- These are three stages of one evidentiary inquiry. A typed sample, shop slips and a shipping label show different ways investigators tried to connect objects to the ransom notes.

### Gaps

- These passages do not establish that either local machine or the Cleveland Underwood typed the ransom notes. They do not identify the ultimate disposition of the recovered typewriter or report a fingerprint match.

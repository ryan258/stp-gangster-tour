# Newspaper dispatches about Wildwood and Sankey

## The gold basketball at Wildwood

### Takeaway

Two contemporary wires followed the discovery of three shooting victims near White Bear Lake. United Press reported that headlights led officers to the car and a miniature gold basketball helped police put a name to Frank Coleman.

### Cited Findings

- A Saint Paul dateline on August 14, 1930 says state investigators were checking a report about George “Bugs” Moran when they noticed a parked car with headlights burning near Wildwood Amusement Park. The report says Coleman was found near the car. The three deaths are also reported in the Associated Press story. [United Press, *Eastland Telegram*, August 14, 1930, printed p. 1, “Trio Believed Slain By Rival Gangster Band”](https://newspapers.swco.ttu.edu/bitstreams/79b8eb67-8519-4b74-ac08-1827772b55c8/download) [Associated Press, *Eugene Guard*, August 14, 1930, printed p. 1, Saint Paul dateline beneath “Guns Take Five Lives”](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn96088108/1930-08-14/ed-1/seq-1/)
- The United Press continuation describes a gold basketball on a chain bearing the inscription “K. C. ’20.” The account presents it as one item used by police in identifying Coleman. [*Eastland Telegram*, printed p. 2, “Trio Believed— (Continued from page 1),” paragraph beginning “A gold basketball”](https://newspapers.swco.ttu.edu/bitstreams/79b8eb67-8519-4b74-ac08-1827772b55c8/download)
- The dispatches named Coleman and Sammy Stein and gave a tentative identification of the third man as “Butch” Myers, a figure from the Saint Paul underworld. Wire versions spell the surname differently. AP reported a police theory that the shootings arose from a quarrel over slot machine concessions. [*Eastland Telegram*, p. 1, tentative names](https://newspapers.swco.ttu.edu/bitstreams/79b8eb67-8519-4b74-ac08-1827772b55c8/download) [*Eugene Guard*, p. 1, Saint Paul dateline and “Killed By Rivals” paragraphs](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn96088108/1930-08-14/ed-1/seq-1/)

### Inferences

- The reader can move from the headlights to the basketball to the competing levels of certainty in the wires. The report of the Moran tip explains the officers’ presence in the area. It supplies no link between Moran and the shootings.

### Gaps

- The early reports leave the third man's legal name, the shooters, the motive and the origin of the gold basketball unresolved. A draft payable to A. Mayers appears in the same report, with no evidence establishing a link to “Butch” Myers.

## The Babe Ruth clipping in Sankey’s file

### Takeaway

A Ramsey County prosecutor described earnings records about Babe Ruth and Jack Dempsey in the search of Verne Sankey’s ranch. The idea that Sankey planned to kidnap them was the prosecutor's inference. Bohn’s testimony and Purvis’s later account of Sankey’s admission supply the distinct Saint Paul kidnapping record.

### Cited Findings

- At the June 1933 opening of Fern Sankey’s trial in Saint Paul, the AP reported that Haskell Bohn named Verne Sankey and Ray Robinson as the men who had taken him from the rear of his home on June 30 the previous summer. Robinson had pleaded guilty. The January 1934 account reported Fern's acquittal. [*Daily Iowan*, June 13, 1933, June 12 Saint Paul dateline, “Testimony Opens in Case Involving Bohn Kidnaping”](https://dailyiowan.lib.uiowa.edu/DI/1933/di1933-06-13.pdf) [*Manchester Evening Herald*, January 3, 1934, AP dispatch from Saint Paul](https://cdn.manchesterhistory.org/News/Manchester%20Evening%20Hearld_1934-01-03.pdf)
- Ramsey County Attorney M. F. Kinkead said a search of Sankey’s ranch at Gann Valley, South Dakota turned up a file about Jack Dempsey’s and Babe Ruth’s earnings. He described a picture of Ruth with a notation at the bottom of the sheet. Kinkead inferred that Sankey intended to kidnap them. The issue masthead and dispatch date are January 3, 1934, weeks before the arrest. [*Manchester Evening Herald*, January 3 Saint Paul dateline and paragraph beginning “We found in the basement”](https://cdn.manchesterhistory.org/News/Manchester%20Evening%20Hearld_1934-01-03.pdf)
- Agents arrested Sankey in a Chicago barber shop on January 31. AP reported on February 1 that Melvin Purvis said Sankey had confessed to taking part in the Bohn kidnapping and the separate Boettcher kidnapping. The AP continued to attribute the claim about Ruth and Dempsey to Kinkead. [*Long Branch Daily Record*, February 1, 1934, continuation “No Sankey Link To Lindbergh Case,” Chicago dateline and paragraphs on the confession and athlete allegations](https://ecode360.com/LO1625/document/430361415.pdf)

### Inferences

- The three dispatches let a reader compare a statement in court, a prosecutor’s claim about his evidence and a federal agent’s statement about an admission. The reported earnings file does not establish how the papers were used.

### Gaps

- The original file and photo are unlocated in these sources. The name of whoever wrote the notation and the existence of any concrete attempt remain unverified. The Manchester copy or its OCR says June 1933 for the kidnapping, but the Daily Iowan’s June 1933 trial account specifies the previous summer.

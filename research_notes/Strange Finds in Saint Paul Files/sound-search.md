# The Bremer sound search

## How did Saint Paul agents look for the hideout?

### Takeaway

Edward Bremer’s remembered overlap of a whistle and a siren sent Saint Paul investigators to town officials, clergy and telephone operators. The surviving file preserves one pastor’s account of 48 tolls and a later exchange about destroying the inquiry circulars.

### Cited Findings

- In a June 12, 1934 letter, Saint Paul inspector H. H. Clegg described Bremer’s memory of a long morning whistle. The siren swelled after the whistle began. The whistle continued briefly after the siren ended. Agent R. E. Newby’s June 7 report says Bremer went out with agents to look for the sounds. A Madison streetcar bell resembled one he recalled from the trip to the hideout. The towns they visited did not match the timing he gave. [June 12 letter, file 7-30, stamped p. 305](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%2048/), [Newby report, file 7-30, synopsis immediately after archive-stamped p. 720](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%2047/at_download/file)
- The Saint Paul office sorted towns by number of sounds that might fit. Some clergy defended the custom of ringing bells. Some civic officials asked whether a new federal law restricted sirens or factory whistles. The office kept a master index and gave telephone operators a questionnaire on simultaneous whistle and siren sounds, their timing and weekend church bells. [June 12 letter, stamped pp. 305–306, and operator questionnaire, stamped p. 75](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%2048/)
- The Rev. Oscar Rockoff of Brookfield, Illinois, wrote to the Saint Paul office in response to a circular. He then consulted his church records for visiting agents. Their March 14 report gives the Saturday evening and Sunday morning schedule and mentions an occasion when the bell was tolled 48 times to mark the death and age of a parishioner. The extracted date row contains a weekday/date mismatch, so the scene omits a precise date for the tolls. [Charles Jenkins report, Chicago file 7-82, “AT BROOKFIELD, ILLINOIS,” January 1934 table and the note on 48 tolls, before stamped p. 130](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%2025/at_download/file)
- Once the hideout had been found, D. M. Ladd wrote to Washington on April 29, 1935 asking permission to destroy the circular letters. Hoover’s May 7 reply authorized it. Both letters survive together. [Bremer case file 7-576-5763, April 29 request and May 7 reply](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20128/)

### Inferences

- The sound pattern, unusual replies and archive decision support three browser scenes: compare the written timing, open the church reply, and open the destruction authorization. A diagram can show order and overlap without asserting durations or playing supposed historical audio.

### Gaps

- These passages give no identification of Rockoff’s bell as a sound Bremer heard. They do not show that the sound search located the hideout. Hoover’s letter establishes permission to destroy the circulars. The extent and date of any destruction remain unresolved.

## A later Rondo gambling case for a possible afterword

### Takeaway

A 1946 search of a home at 411 Rondo Avenue produced a dream book, a red notebook and tissue carbon copies. The Minnesota Supreme Court reversed the resulting conviction in 1948.

### Cited Findings

- Officers described the book as a guide to numbers for the lottery game known as policy. The court describes red and green columns in the tally book and numbers on tissue carbon copies. It accepted that the papers could function as gambling devices and found the evidence insufficient to prove the defendant’s wrongful, willful possession. The dream book bore his wife’s name. [*City of St. Paul v. Stovall*, 225 Minn. 309, 30 N.W.2d 638, ¶¶ 19–25, pp. 314–317](https://openjurist.org/225/minn/309/city-of-st-paul-v-stovall)
- The court rejected the challenge to the search on the record before it. The reversal turned on sufficiency of the possession evidence. [*Stovall*, ¶¶ 7–15, 22–25, pp. 310–313 and 316–317](https://openjurist.org/225/minn/309/city-of-st-paul-v-stovall)

### Inferences

- The three paper exhibits could make an evidence table if a future edition extends into the 1940s.

### Gaps

- The opinion does not establish a gang connection. Its dates fall outside the Casebook’s focus on the 1900–1938 era.

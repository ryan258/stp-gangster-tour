# Two clues found in ordinary places

## The ransom money under the hatband

Hoover’s April 30, 1934 memorandum for the Attorney General, first sheet, describes the Uptown State Bank assistant cashier checking five-dollar serials from a repeat customer against the Bremer list and finding a match. The Chicago circular led to a second bank report at First National on April 26 and William Vidler’s arrest. The Chicago field narrative, printed pp. 48–49, says Jack McLaughlin first denied wearing a hat, tried a gray felt about a size and a half too large, then pointed out a brown flat-brimmed felt hat. The agent found two sealed envelopes under its sweatband. The field count, its later comparison with the ransom list, and Hoover’s summary give differing amounts for cash found and identified. Both records report a partial ransom match. [Bremer Kidnapping Part 43, April 30 memo and field narrative](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%2043/at_download/file)

Saint Paul U.S. attorney George F. Sullivan’s June 25 letter and Melvin Purvis’s June 26 letter describe Vidler’s request for $460 and say the sum could not be identified as ransom. Purvis wanted the agent who had counted and sealed it to reopen it if it was returned. The reproduced petition, at archive stamps 233–235, describes a $560 balance and alleges $100 had already been paid to Vidler’s wife. The letters give the resulting disputed balance. The sources reviewed give no final order on the money. [Bremer Kidnapping Part 54, archive stamps 186–188 and 233–235](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%2054/at_download/file)

The Bremer summary records Vidler’s acquittal and John J. McLaughlin Sr.’s conviction in the Saint Paul jury verdict on May 17, 1935. This is a different outcome from the cash petition. The current Casebook has other Bremer episodes about the ransom handoff, hideout investigation, typewriters and the subsequent trial. None address the cash exchanges, hat search or petition. [Bremer Investigation Summary, printed pp. 44–45, S10](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/at_download/file)

## The Buick spotted from a streetcar

Paul R. Gold’s “The Legend of Sam Taran” dates Taran’s appearance before Judge Hugo Hanft to April 23, 1921. Gold says the owner of a $1,400 Buick roadster saw it from a Saint Paul streetcar at University and Dale, recognized brochures from his nut-shelling plant inside, and called police. Gold reports Taran was released and quotes the *St. Paul Daily News* on the judge’s advice to stick to tailoring. The original newspaper page, brochures and docket remain unverified. This story concerns the 1921 accusation and release. The existing Casebook story about a furnace concerns a 1927 car finance transaction and the ensuing larceny conviction. [Gold, printed p. 14, “The Car Thief,” first two paragraphs](https://rchs.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/RCHS_Winter1998_Gold.pdf#page=4)

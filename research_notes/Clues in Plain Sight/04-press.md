# Press accounts

## What happened after Danny Hogan was taken to the hospital?

### Cited findings

- A December 4, 1928 Saint Paul dateline says Hogan died after surgery for injuries from a bomb that exploded when he stepped on his car’s starter. The wire describes him as a bootlegger and underworld mediator. [Daily Iowan, “Underworld Leader Dies From Injuries,” December 5, 1928, front page](https://dailyiowan.lib.uiowa.edu/DI/1928/di1928-12-05.pdf)
- Curt Brown reports that bootleggers, gamblers, racketeers and a boxing champion turned up at the hospital to offer blood. He also quotes the *Minneapolis Morning Tribune* predicting fewer turkey dinners in Saint Paul that Christmas. The Calvary Cemetery registration form, sections 9–end p. 35, note 45, identifies the *Morning Tribune* death story as “Danny Hogan Slain; Victim of Auto Bomb,” December 5, 1928, p. 1. Brown’s account is the available source for the Christmas wording and the hospital volunteers. [Star Tribune, April 28, 2018](https://www.startribune.com/1928-car-bombing-that-killed-st-paul-mob-boss-dapper-dan-hogan-still-unsolved/481153181), [Calvary Cemetery registration form, note 45](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/CLG%20%26%20SRB_Calvary%20Cemetery_nomination_tcm36-768759.pdf)
- Paul R. Gold’s history of the St. Francis Hotel describes more than 2,000 mourners and five carloads of flowers. Gold attributes the recollection about seeing Granada Club regulars at St. Mary’s Catholic Church to Harry Turgeon. [Ramsey County History, “Rats, Politicians and Librarians,” Winter 1997, p. 9, paragraph before “Epilogue”](https://rchs.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/RCHS_Winter1997_Gold.pdf)

### Overlap and limits

The main tour mentions Hogan’s role as an intermediary and his death in the 1928 bombing. The dispatch story starts at the hospital and follows the local reaction through the funeral. The reporting establishes offers of blood and leaves individual donors and completed transfusions unresolved. The Christmas line expresses the newspaper’s view of Hogan’s reputation, and the funeral attendance and flowers are figures from a later local history. [Main tour stops](../../src/data/stops.json)

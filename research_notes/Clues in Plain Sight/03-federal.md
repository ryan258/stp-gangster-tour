# The $2 taxi errand and the Lincoln Court coat

## A two-dollar delivery

- A June 17 United Press dispatch reports that a taxi driver carried two notes to Hamm Brewing manager William Dunn’s Saint Paul home, where waiting police arrested him. The driver said he received $2 and was unaware of the contents. The dispatch also describes his tentative reaction to a photograph of Verne Sankey. [The Bend Bulletin, June 17, 1933, front page, Saint Paul dateline and paragraphs on the Dunn home and Sankey photograph](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn90051770/1933-06-17/ed-1/seq-1.pdf)
- Item 97 identifies the driver as Leo J. Allison. According to its description of a 1933 press photograph, Allison said defendant Eddie McFadden resembled the sender, without positively identifying him. The description is the basis for the courtroom detail. [Kent State University Libraries, Crime Photographs collection, item 97, ‘Hamm, William A. Jr. 1933’](https://www.library.kent.edu/special-collections-and-archives/crime-photographs-collection)
- The Associated Press reported that the jury acquitted McFadden, Roger Touhy, Willie Sharkey and Gustav Schaefer of transporting Hamm across state lines and conspiring to do so. The Casebook uses this dispatch as S102. [Medford Mail Tribune, November 28, 1933, front page, ‘Fail to Convict Touhy Gangsters’](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn97071090/1933-11-28/ed-1/seq-1/ocr/)

The dispatch format follows the taxi driver through the delivery, his response to the police photograph, and the courtroom and verdict. The early wire report leaves the driver unnamed. The archive description names him and summarizes his testimony without a transcript. The two accounts support a comparison of tentative responses rather than a claim that Allison changed a positive identification. The existing Touhy story follows courtroom security and the verdict. The existing Bohn package story concerns a different kidnapping and delivery.

## The coat left in 303

- The outline for Werner Hanni’s expected testimony lists a woman’s light tan coat with a platinum fox collar and a Young-Quinlan Company label among the effects found in Apartment 303 at Lincoln Court. The entry identifies no owner or purchaser. [FBI Vault, Dillinger Gang Part 12, witness outline near the end of the volume, after the printed p. 5 marker](https://vault.fbi.gov/John%20Dillinger%20/john/John%20Dillinger%20Gang%20Part%2012)
- Patricia Cherrington told federal agents she, her sister and John Hamilton contacted Homer Van Meter at a Saint Paul restaurant after the Lincoln Court shooting. She said he took them to Harry Sawyer’s cottage near Saint Paul, where they spent four days before word of a raid sent them back to the city. [FBI Vault, Bremer Kidnapping Part 145, opening page, paragraph beginning ‘PATRICIA CHERRINGTON’](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20145)
- Bessie Skinner recounted a story she said Van Meter told her. Her account places the trio at McCormick’s restaurant after they turned away from Lincoln Court. She described their ride in a Ford sedan and said she believed their destination was Sawyer’s farm. [FBI Vault, Bremer Kidnapping Part 175, Bessie Skinner interview, paragraph beginning ‘According to information Mrs. Skinner received from Van Meter’](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20175/at_download/file)

The comparison starts with the coat label and puts a participant’s route next to a secondhand route. The file supplies the label and the two attributed accounts. The records reviewed for this story supply no receipt, link between Cherrington and the coat, or exact site for the cottage. Existing Lincoln Court stories cover the shooting, a car purchase, the telegram and Van Meter’s reported soap-sales cover story. The coat and the women’s accounts of the retreat supply the new material.

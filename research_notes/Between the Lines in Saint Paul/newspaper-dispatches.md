# Newspaper dispatches

## What happened when the Touhy defendants reached Saint Paul?

### Takeaway

Three stops frame the 1933 federal Hamm proceedings from the defendants’ point of arrival to the jury’s verdict. The Associated Press described a special car on a mail train, a hearing guarded with machine guns in the jail basement, and tear gas guns in the courtroom when the trial opened. On November 28, the jury acquitted all four defendants on the two federal Hamm charges.

### Cited findings

- The August 19 Saint Paul dateline reports a Milwaukee mail train arriving at 2:45 p.m. with a special car attached. Eddie McFadden and Gus Schaefer disembarked cuffed together, as did Roger Touhy and Willie Sharkey. Officers took the men to jail under guard. [Daily Iowan, “Touhy, Gang, Taken to St. Paul Prison Under Heavy Guard,” August 20, 1933, printed p. 6](https://dailyiowan.lib.uiowa.edu/DI/1933/di1933-08-20.pdf)
- Federal judges J. W. Molyneaux and M. M. Joyce came to the jail for the September 25 motions hearing so the prisoners could avoid a street transfer. The final paragraph places the hearing in the basement with machine-gun guards and the prisoners’ cells upstairs. The court declined to separate the trials and left bail at $100,000 per defendant. [Daily Iowan, “Touhy Denied Bail Reduction,” September 26, 1933, printed p. 3](https://dailyiowan.lib.uiowa.edu/DI/1933/di1933-09-26.pdf)
- The November 9 Saint Paul dateline reports deputy sheriffs with tear gas guns in the courtroom and checks for passes in the courthouse’s eighth-floor corridors. The “Curious Turned Back” paragraphs describe people being denied entry. [Brownsville Herald, “Armed Guards Circle Room at St. Paul,” November 9, 1933, printed p. 1](https://texashistory.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metapth1394768/m1/1/)
- The November 28 Saint Paul dateline reports the acquittals and describes the two Hamm counts as transporting the kidnap victim across state lines and conspiring to transport him. The story separately reports charges relating to John Factor. [Medford Mail Tribune, “Fail to Convict Touhy Gangsters,” November 28, 1933, printed p. 1](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn97071090/1933-11-28/ed-1/seq-1/ocr/)

### Inferences

A chronological sequence lets readers follow how security changed with the setting and ends with the outcome of the federal case. The three dates describe a transfer, a motions hearing and the trial.

### Gaps

The details of the guards and transport come from contemporary dispatches. The exact routes between the station, jail and courthouse are unconfirmed.

## What did the papers and later county history record about Rutman’s kidnapping?

### Takeaway

An October 8, 1931 dispatch called Rutman a women’s dress shop proprietor and placed his captivity near White Bear Lake. A later history of the Ramsey County Attorney’s Office identified him as a bootlegger and named a Mahtomedi gambling spot. Both descriptions belong in the story with their sources visible.

### Cited findings

- The October 8 Saint Paul dispatch identifies Moritz Rutman, 27, by his occupation and says he was held three days at a cottage near White Bear Lake. It reports his release early that morning, his return home and his hospitalization. Its closing sentence attributes the $50,000 demand to Rutman. [Pampa Daily News, “Kidnaped Man Beaten Before Being Released,” October 8, 1931, printed p. 1](https://newspapers.swco.ttu.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/9b93ceed-2dfb-4fb1-972a-4afcf815f9ee/content)
- A longer dispatch from the same dateline carries a denial of payment from the police chief and a report from friends that relatives paid. The paragraph says this second report could not be verified. The relevant passages fall between the demand and the “Was Abducted” subhead. The scan clips portions of the item, and the overlapping dispatches carry the same morning’s reporting. [Brownwood Banner-Bulletin, October 8, 1931, Saint Paul dispatch on Moritz Rutman’s release](https://newspapers.swco.ttu.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/14e6a650-8275-481e-b08f-539ddb75bdb3/content)
- Anne E. Cowie names Morris Rutman, Jack Ferrick and the Silver Slipper in the “Organized Crime” section, immediately above the paragraph introducing the Abe Wagner prosecution. She writes that Ferrick and associates took Rutman from his Saint Paul home and held him at the Silver Slipper, a Mahtomedi gambling spot. Note 25 cites Paul Maccabee’s *John Dillinger Slept Here*, p. 113. [Ramsey County History, “Two Horses and One Buffalo Robe,” Fall 2000, printed p. 12 and note 25](https://rchs.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/RCHS_Fall2000_Cowie.pdf)

### Inferences

The two sources appear to use different given names for the same man, based on the shared surname, month, Saint Paul abduction and general area of captivity. A side-by-side treatment can show the job title and the later underworld account with both names visible. A final panel can give the reported ransom demand and the differing accounts of payment.

### Gaps

Whether ransom changed hands remains unresolved. The full headline and printed page of the longer dispatch are illegible in the available scan. The exact relationship between the reported cottage and the Silver Slipper, and whether the dress shop had a role in Rutman’s bootlegging, remain unconfirmed.

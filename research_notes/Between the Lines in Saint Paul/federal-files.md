# Two trails through the federal files

## The typewriter on the supply shelf

### Takeaway

The search for the Bremer ransom typewriter took investigators through Saint Paul repair records. A separate machine recovered from a gang apartment turned up in the Bureau’s supply room.

### Cited findings

- The February 26, 1934 Saint Paul memo says to type three sample lines, including the quick brown fox sentence, on paper similar to the ransom notes. It describes the machine sought as a 1929 portable Corona with pica type. It also notes that a Corona No. 3 that once had a slightly smudgy purple ribbon did not appear to be the match. [Bremer Kidnapping Part 17, February 26 memorandum, archive-stamped pp. 107–108](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%2017)
- Agent J. E. Brennan says the repair records were filed by customer name. Among about 500 slips from eight months of records, investigators located two for the type of machine they sought. One was a portable from a boiler and machinery company on Robert Street with a $1.50 repair bill. The other serial matches the purple-ribbon entry. [Barker Karpis Part 77, Brennan’s “Re: Typewriters” memorandum, immediately before archive stamp 320](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/barker-karpis/Barker%20Karpis%20Part%2077)
- The September 27 memorandum describes a recovered Underwood from a Cleveland apartment occupied by Alvin Karpis and others. The Chief Clerk’s Office held it as Bureau property and a clerk discovered it on a supply-room shelf in a case with a painted room number. The October 2 letter says the laboratory shipping labels were missing. The lab reported difficulty obtaining samples and had tentatively identified a portable Corona for the Hamm and Bremer ransom notes. [Bremer Kidnapping Part 59, archive-stamped pp. 239–241](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%2059/)

### Inferences

- The test lines, repair slips, and shipping trail give readers three ways to inspect a case lead. They are paraphrased from the records except for the three transcribed test lines.

### Gaps

- The cited reports do not identify the machine that typed either case’s notes. They give no result of the proposed check for latent prints on the Underwood.

## The man who said he sold soap

### Takeaway

An early federal memo preserves the soap-sales cover story and an uncertain identification. Bessie Skinner later described a conversation with Van Meter, while the Bureau’s retrospective identifies him as the man in the hallway.

### Cited findings

- An April 8, 1934 memorandum for the Director records Clegg saying Coulter thought Van Meter might be the man at the Lexington Avenue apartment. It notes resemblance between Van Meter and Green in photographs, no Van Meter fingerprints then found in the places and objects examined, and the woman who provided the information did not see the hallway encounter. [Dillinger Gang Part 12, April 8 memorandum, first page, beginning with Clegg’s afternoon call](https://vault.fbi.gov/John%20Dillinger%20/john/John%20Dillinger%20Gang%20Part%2012)
- Bessie Skinner told investigators Van Meter later visited the apartment she shared with Eddie Green and said he had presented himself as a soap salesman and fired at the officer who questioned him. [Dillinger Gang Part 12, Skinner interview, printed p. 10, passage after her account of the Marshall Avenue apartment](https://vault.fbi.gov/John%20Dillinger%20/john/John%20Dillinger%20Gang%20Part%2012)
- The FBI’s modern case account identifies Van Meter as the man in the hall who drew a gun and fled after shots were exchanged. It omits the soap detail. [FBI case history, “The Bureau Actively Joins the Hunt”](https://www.fbi.gov/history/cases-and-criminals/john-dillinger)

### Inferences

- Pairing the April 8 memo with Skinner’s interview allows readers to compare a tentative identification with an account of a later conversation.

### Gaps

- The cited interview reports what Skinner said she heard. The exact hallway conversation and the escape vehicle’s particular cargo are outside these sources.

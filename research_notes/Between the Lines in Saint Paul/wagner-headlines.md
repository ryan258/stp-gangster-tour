# Abe Wagner’s premature death headline

## The July 13 dispatch

The July 13, 1932 *Manchester Evening Herald* in Connecticut carried a short Associated Press item headed “Abie Wagner Slain” on printed page 2. The AP attributed the claim to the *New York Sun*. The dispatch said Wagner had been killed about two months earlier and his body had been sunk in the East River in a barrel of cement. This is direct evidence of what the newspaper published. The Saint Paul chronology contradicts the dispatch’s account of his death. The archive filename preserves the archive’s spelling `Hearld`, and the OCR sometimes reads the date’s final digit as 8. The issue is the July 13 Wednesday edition. [Manchester Historical Society scan, p. 2](https://cdn.manchesterhistory.org/News/Manchester%20Evening%20Hearld_1932-07-13.pdf#page=2)

## The July 25 shooting and the Saint Paul case

Anne E. Cowie’s Fall 2000 county history dates the shooting to July 25, 1932, places it at University and Snelling avenues, and describes Wagner as a bootlegger from the East Coast who posed as a fruit peddler. The following section, “Top Legal Talent,” says George Young and Joseph Schaefer received life sentences. Endnote 26 on printed page 16 cites Paul Maccabee’s *John Dillinger Slept Here*, pp. 53–54. The fruit-selling cover is attributed to Cowie. [Ramsey County History, printed p. 12](https://rchs.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/RCHS_Fall2000_Cowie.pdf#page=11)

T. F. Cullen’s July 22, 1933 letter from the Bureau of Investigation’s New York office to its Kansas City office, file 62-4949, p. 2, reports the July 26, 1932 Saint Paul arrests of Albert L. Silverburg and John Newman for the murder of Abe Wagner. Locate the paragraph beginning “The St. Paul Office will observe.” The letter also reports life sentences. The pair of names differs from Cowie’s, and the federal letter gives a different prison location. These sources establish that the federal office recognized the victim as Abe Wagner by July 1933. They leave the pairwise alias mapping, the exact prison location in this comparison, and the original identification procedure unsettled. The letter itself is dated July 22. The receiving stamp is July 25, 1933. [Federal Bureau of Investigation Vault, Kansas City Massacre Part 11](https://vault.fbi.gov/Kansas%20City%20Massacre/Kansas%20City%20Massacre%20Part%2011/)

The Minnesota Historical Society’s Maccabee finding aid lists three George Young and Joey Schaefer folders with Minnesota District Court indictments, an FBI investigative memorandum, and state prison records. The catalog establishes that the folders exist. The underlying pages would be needed to verify any alias matching or prison privileges from original records. [Paul Maccabee papers finding aid, entry “Young, George and Joey Schaefer”](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/00239.html)

# Gang files behind Saint Paul Hijinks in Old Records

## Dillinger’s Hudson and the black wire wheels

**Casebook.** `dillingers-hudson-wire-wheels`, claims C120–C122, sources S76 and S11.

- **The $331 account.** The federal report’s printed pp. 16–17 record W. J. Potthoff’s account of the March 28–29, 1934 negotiation at 216 Bates Avenue in Saint Paul. He said he offered $331 for the Chevrolet under the trade code and that Irving Gleeman accepted that figure. He also said Gleeman requested black wire wheels and agreed to take the Hudson before the wheels were changed. [Dillinger Gang section 10, S76](https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/fbifiles/gangsterera/johndillinger/dillinger%20gang%20section%2010.pdf)
- **The $375 account.** The same file’s printed pp. 18–20 record Gleeman’s interview. He agreed that the offer began at $331 and said it rose to $375. He said Tom Filben supplied the name for the order and that he told Potthoff to put down any address. In the question-and-answer passage just before the printed p. 21 marker, Gleeman said the dealer credited his $206 check as $250 and attributed the $44 difference to the allowance above the code price. The federal file renders the assumed surname several ways. The FBI’s case history uses Hellman for Dillinger and Frechette’s Lincoln Court tenancy. [Dillinger Gang section 10, S76](https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/fbifiles/gangsterera/johndillinger/dillinger%20gang%20section%2010.pdf) · [FBI case history, S11, “The Bureau Actively Joins the Hunt”](https://www.fbi.gov/history/cases-and-criminals/john-dillinger)
- **The transfer agreement.** In the passage immediately before the printed p. 50 marker, Agent E. N. Notestein dates his Potthoff interview to April 2 and records a March 29 sale and delivery, with $375 allowed on the transfer agreement. The introductory paragraph identifies the Hudson as the one in Dillinger’s possession when he escaped. The file describes the agreement. The accounting rationale is Gleeman’s explanation, and the records cited here do not trace every handoff to Dillinger. [Dillinger Gang section 10, S76](https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/fbifiles/gangsterera/johndillinger/dillinger%20gang%20section%2010.pdf)

The printed markers and passage text are verified in the indexed scan. The exact PDF viewer page offsets remain unverified.

## Four mail pouches and a Plymouth key

**Casebook.** `garrettsville-mail-pouches-and-key`, claims C132–C134, sources S84 and S10.

- **The circular.** The paragraph beginning “A circular issued by the Post Office Department” records the November 7, 1935 holdup at the Erie station in Garrettsville, Ohio. It says two men entered the mail car and took two registered-mail pouches and two ordinary-mail pouches. The period accounts differ on the train number and the total number of robbers. [Bremer Kidnapping Part 179, S84](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20179/at_download/file)
- **The inspector’s lead.** In the December 16, 1935 interview, a postal inspector described a gunmetal gray 1935 Plymouth with two windshield wipers, two taillights, a trunk and key no. MP 802. The inspectors suspected Alvin Karpis and Harry Campbell at that stage. Inspector Casey separately reported that a witness had identified Nelson, alias Coker. The record does not establish that the key number led to an identification. [Bremer Kidnapping Part 179, S84, inspector interviews immediately above the circular](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20179/at_download/file)
- **The Saint Paul connection.** The November 19, 1936 FBI summary, printed p. 51, says investigators later identified Karpis and Campbell as participants in the Ohio robbery and identifies them as fugitives wanted in the Saint Paul Bremer case. That passage reports the Bureau’s conclusion. It gives no conviction or disposition on the mail robbery. [Bremer Investigation Summary, S10, printed p. 51](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/at_download/file)

## Reserve lead. The apartment phone Jesse Doyle described

- The November 11, 1935 letter marked “Personal and Confidential,” file 7-576-8042, records Special Agent G. H. Wilson’s November 7 interview with Doyle at the Nebraska State Penitentiary. On printed p. 1, Doyle says the gang reserved a telephone in a Saint Paul apartment for tips from someone at the State Apprehension Bureau at the Capitol. He says one call went to Harry Sawyer’s place when nobody answered. The answer continues on p. 2. Doyle says he does not know who the caller was and gives only a physical description. [Bremer Kidnapping Part 171, printed pp. 1–2 of the November 11 letter](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20171/)
- The document establishes that Doyle made the allegation. The arrangement, caller, address and connection to any specific police action remain unconfirmed in this record. The episode is distinct from the court-documented Raasch recordings and the news-service rumor about Karpis’s arrest.

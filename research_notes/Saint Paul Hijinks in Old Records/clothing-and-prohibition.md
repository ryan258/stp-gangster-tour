# Clothing trace and Prohibition premises

## The shirt with no label

- **Release and lab work (C126).** The February 27 Saint Paul field report says the abductors gave Bremer a shirt, socks and underwear at release. Report 360 describes the laboratory examination requested by the Saint Paul office: a missing shirt label with holes where the thread had been, a collar button 0.3989 inches across, a blue crayon marking and the gathered heel seams on the socks. It does not identify who took out the label or bought the clothes. [Lab report, February 14, 1934, archive-stamped pp. 121–122 (S80)](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/barker-karpis/Barker%20Karpis%20Part%2074), [Saint Paul report, February 27, archive-stamped p. 128 (S81)](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%2017)
- **Two possible shops (C127).** The Saint Paul report cross-indexed possible retailers for the garments. It listed Mueller Clothing Company in Windom, Minnesota, and J. H. Conley in Watertown, South Dakota, for the shirt and underwear. This was an investigative index with no receipt. [February 27 report, archive-stamped p. 128 (S81)](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%2017)
- **The Windom check (C128).** W. C. Ryan interviewed Gustave Mueller on March 5. The shopkeeper said his shirt was a lighter color and different style, his order for the relevant underwear had been filled with another brand, and the shop did not handle the socks. The memo addresses Windom; it does not resolve the place of purchase. [Ryan’s March 5 memorandum for Saint Paul file 7-30, before the March 6 memorandum (S82)](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/barker-karpis/Barker%20Karpis%20Part%2077)

This inquiry is separate from the Casebook story about the wallpaper and strawberry shortcake. The browser trace follows a material clue from examination to list to interview, and shows the limitation of the apparent match.

## A new lease, the same bar

**C129–C131.** At 905 West Seventh Street the first floor was used as a soft drink bar. Federal agents described saloon fittings. The parties stipulated to four April 6, 1925 sales and liquor possession on April 15. John Ryshavy took over May 1. He had not been charged with a violation, but the appeals court found the same equipment remained and the landlord had not shown that the unlawful use had stopped. The court affirmed a civil order closing the premises for one year from April 11, 1927. The case establishes no organized crime link or new liquor sale by the replacement tenant. [*Engler v. United States*, 25 F.2d 37 (8th Cir. 1928), pp. 38–39 (S83)](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/25/37/1548515/)

The Casebook timeline follows the address through sales, replacement tenant and appeal. It uses the lease and the remaining fixtures as the comparison points.

## Regional reserve

The [*Osage County News*, September 5, 1924, image 7](https://gateway.okhistory.org/ark:/67531/metadc1599493/m1/7/) identifies Florence and Susie Freiermuth beside distilling equipment, with a caption alleging an armed encounter. [WCCO’s 2024 family account](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/minnesota-farm-at-the-center-of-american-history/) places the August 14 raid near Hampton in Dakota County and says the women posed at the agents’ request. The image does not establish what happened before the pose. The location and absence of a mob connection made this a weaker fit for the Saint Paul batch.

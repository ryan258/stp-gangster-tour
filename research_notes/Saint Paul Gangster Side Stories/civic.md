# Police protection, vice, and the political machine

## Which stories would add a new thread to the tour?

### Takeaway

Start with the Green Lantern photos, Nina Clifford’s excavated brothel, and Richard O’Connor’s wager. Each has an object visitors can examine or learn to read. Hall’s barber story adds a civilian witness to the protection system. Peifer provides a direct continuation of the Hamm story with conflicting accounts of his last hours.

### Cited Findings

- **A shooting, three booths, and a pork chop sandwich.** On March 19, 1931, John Quinn shot Frank Ventress behind the Green Lantern on Wabasha between Tenth and College. Quinn claimed self-defense. A jury convicted him of second-degree murder and the Minnesota Supreme Court affirmed in 1932. In 1934 the court upheld denial of a new trial after witness Harry Kremer gave a sworn account repudiating much of his testimony. The earlier opinion found Quinn’s own account and other evidence sufficient for the jury to reject self-defense. Quinn worked for Minneapolis gambler Zip Royan, and the opinion explicitly says the suggestion he had Chicago gangster ties went unestablished. [State v. Quinn, 186 Minn. 242, facts pp. 243–44, discussion pp. 249–53](https://law.counselstack.com/opinion/state-v-quinn-minn-1932), [State v. Quinn, 192 Minn. 88, pp. 88–90](https://calculators.law/caselaw/decisions/0y48qy7bE8zx/state-v-quinn).
- In 2019, reporter Nick Woltman followed local researcher Jeff Neuberger’s attempt to identify a water-damaged envelope of photographs and negatives found at the Saint Paul Police Historical Society. A synagogue pillar finial appeared in an exterior photo. Three booths and the spaghetti and pork chop sandwich on the menu board matched witness descriptions of the cafe, and Woltman cites 1931 trial reporting that Kremer identified police photos of the rear entrance and interior. Several photos were dated May 1931 with case mark X-40. One exterior view marked X-40 bore May 1930, which Neuberger proposed was a labeling error. These are converging clues to an identification, with the exterior date an unresolved discrepancy. The original Pioneer Press URL would not open in this pass, but the attributed reproduction was accessible. [Woltman, Pioneer Press, April 13, 2019, reproduced with images](https://twincitiesmusichighlights.net/venue/green-lantern-i/).
- **What the dig found under Washington Street.** Nina Clifford ran a brothel at 147 South Washington Street in Saint Paul. In 1997, construction monitoring for the Science Museum relocation uncovered its foundation walls and artifacts, alongside another brothel at no. 149. The ensuing Washington Street District investigation found material differences between customers’ and residents’ lives. Its authors describe contrasts in vessels and animal remains and evidence of residents’ poor health. Contemporary reporting described the hidden diamonds and secret passage as rumors being tested. MNopedia reports that the excavation found no tunnel to the Minnesota Club. [Minnesota archaeology report abstracts, “Archaeological Monitoring For The Science Museum Relocation/Expansion Project”](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/1998abstracts_tcm36-187121.pdf), [Ketz, Abel and Schmidt, Historical Archaeology 39(1), pp. 74–88, abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF03376678), [MPR report from the excavation, April 28, 1997](https://archive.mpr.org/stories/1997/04/28/st-paul-dig-unearths-remnants-red-light), [MNopedia biography, tunnel and bribery passages](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/person/clifford-nina-1851-1929).
- MNopedia describes Clifford’s establishment as the largest in its district by 1895 and states that vice, including prostitution and gambling, was exempt from O’Connor’s local ban. It dates Clifford drawing attention for paying police to 1914. A separate MNopedia biography explains Ida Dorsey’s role as a prosecution witness and cites the February 11, 1914 Minneapolis Morning Tribune story “‘Pay No Graft Money,’ Flanagan Told Woman.” For visitor copy, anchor Clifford to the excavation and the documented system of police protection first. [Clifford biography](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/person/clifford-nina-1851-1929), [Dorsey biography and bibliography](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/person/dorsey-ida-1866-1918).
- **A political bet with a death clause.** On April 9, 1908, a handwritten wager on Merchants Hotel stationery records $1,000 from Richard T. O’Connor, the police chief’s younger brother and a Democratic political boss, on David W. Faulder becoming mayor and $600 from Republican industrialist Philip W. Herzog on Joseph McKibbin. The agreement voided the wager if either bettor or either candidate died before the election. Herzog signed it and the note was addressed to Pierce Butler, who later joined the U.S. Supreme Court. The receipt is MNHS P1190-27, reproduced on printed p. 23. [Minnesota History, spring 2026, pp. 19, 21–23 and note 11](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-org-support/mn_history_articles/70/v70i01p018-028.pdf).
- **A barber for fugitives.** S. Edward Hall’s barbershop sat near City Hall. Hall recalled in a 1972 interview with Steve Trimble that a police carriage took him and his barber kit to locations where fugitives under police protection could get groomed. His recollection identifies license inspector Louis Jessrang. The historian’s footnote says the interview transcript spells the name “Jessering,” while contemporary newspaper references corroborate the spelling Jessrang and his job. The interview is OH 53 at the Gale Family Library. The same article identifies the barbershop in the Germania Bank building near Fifth and Wabasha, and documents Hall’s civic work at the Hallie Q. Brown Community Center and with the Saint Paul Urban League. [Minnesota History, spring 2026, pp. 18–20 and notes 2–3](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-org-support/mn_history_articles/70/v70i01p018-028.pdf).
- **Jack Peifer’s last two hours.** The July 31, 1936 Associated Press dispatch reported John Peter “Jack” Peifer was found on his Ramsey County jail cot after receiving a 30-year sentence in the William Hamm kidnapping case. Doctors judged he had poisoned himself but could not immediately say what poison. A 2021 Star Tribune account places Peifer swallowing cyanide in court when Judge M.M. Joyce denied his bid for a new trial and describes a witness who glimpsed a small white object. It says Peifer had run Saint Paul’s Hollyhocks Club and recounts his role in the plot. The sources converge on conviction and death shortly after sentencing, while the AP report emphasizes the jail and the later reconstruction places the ingestion in court. AP reports doctors pronounced him dead at the hospital. The later account says he died in his cell. [Associated Press, Capital Journal, July 31, 1936, p. 1, “Peifer Kills Self by Poison”](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn90066132/1936-07-31/ed-1/seq-1/), [Curt Brown, Star Tribune, April 3, 2021](https://www.startribune.com/st-paul-gangster-s-kidnapping-trial-came-to-dramatic-end/600041932), [Minnesota BCA inventory, “Peifer, John Peter (No. 52522)”](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/pubsaf08.html).

### Inferences

- “The Arrangement” already introduces the Green Lantern and John O’Connor, “Under the Bluff” covers Castle Royal, and “The Price of Protection” covers the Hamm kidnapping. The five episodes above are absent from current visitor data. The “On the Record” chapter, evidence ledger, and studies already cover the 1935 police wiretap and June 1936 charter amendment. [Working-tree stop data](../../src/data/stops.json), [Working-tree evidence](../../src/data/evidence.json), [Evidence standards](../../docs/evidence-ledger.md).
- Green Lantern makes the strongest first Casebook side story because the original crime is independently documented and the photos let readers weigh a separate question. It can link to “The Arrangement” and end with the mismatched date. Clifford’s artifacts are the strongest contrast to Castle Royal guest lore. The wager works as a short standalone artifact and introduces the role of the political machine before Prohibition. Hall gives the layover a human scale with an attributed firsthand recollection. Peifer has a natural link from the Hamm chapter and could use a two-account treatment. [Working-tree stop data](../../src/data/stops.json), [Current Casebook layout](../../src/pages/casebook.astro).

### Gaps

- **Ventress:** No original negative envelope, exhibit list, or reproduction rights reviewed. Avoid stating the photographs were definitively the exhibits or treating a theory about Quinn’s Chicago ties as fact. Court testimony mentions the Hollyhocks, which may provide a sourced cross-link to Peifer after passage review. [State v. Quinn, pp. 244–49](https://law.counselstack.com/opinion/state-v-quinn-minn-1932), [2019 investigation](https://twincitiesmusichighlights.net/venue/green-lantern-i/).
- **Clifford:** The tunnel absence refers to the excavated site. The alleged path of a chandelier is unresolved in MNopedia. The underlying Clifford bribery court record and contemporary reports were not retrieved here. Lead with the physical findings and say where the tunnel story came from only after locating the source. [MNopedia biography](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/person/clifford-nina-1851-1929).
- **Wager:** The receipt shows the terms, neither whether money changed hands nor electoral manipulation. Butler was the addressee, and the record examined here establishes no active role beyond that. Research separately if using the election result or treating both offices as involved in the bet. [Minnesota History, pp. 22–23 and note 11](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-org-support/mn_history_articles/70/v70i01p018-028.pdf).
- **Hall:** Direct 1972 recording or transcript not retrieved. The similarly cataloged David Vassar Taylor interview is a different 1970 interview. Keep the 1972 account attributed, and avoid claiming identities, precise trips, or motivations the source does not establish. [Minnesota History, notes 1 and 3](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-org-support/mn_history_articles/70/v70i01p018-028.pdf).
- **Peifer:** No death certificate, toxicology or trial transcript examined. Use “died after being found in his cell” for the core. Present the courtroom scene as the Star Tribune’s reconstruction, and skip the speculative murder theory. [AP period report](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn90066132/1936-07-31/ed-1/seq-1/), [Star Tribune reconstruction](https://www.startribune.com/st-paul-gangster-s-kidnapping-trial-came-to-dramatic-end/600041932).
- **Reserve cases:** Alice McQuillan Dunn’s 1917 murder for hire at 793 Selby has a strong primary record, including the testimony about a staged burglary, a payment dispute and the affirmed conviction of her husband. It is adjacent to organized crime and better for a serious crime or investigations feature than for hijinks. Abe Wagner, a New York bootlegger posing as a fruit peddler, was shot in Saint Paul in July 1932, and Ramsey County History recounts the conviction of George Young and Joseph Schaefer and claims that they bribed prison officials to receive steak and whiskey. Both those prison details and the claimed Meyer Lansky commission merit direct record checks before use. [State v. Dunn, 140 Minn. 308](https://openjurist.org/140/minn/308/state-v-dunn), [MNHS Dunn papers notice](https://www2.mnhs.org/library/findaids/index-WhatsNew-November2018.htm), [Ramsey County History, fall 2000, pp. 11–12 and note 26](https://rchs.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/RCHS_Fall2000_Cowie.pdf).

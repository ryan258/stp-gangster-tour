# Saint Paul gangster capers and escapes

## Which local episodes could become standalone stories?

### Takeaway

The true crime magazine that exposed the tenants in West St. Paul, the woman who escaped prison three times and later appeared in a Saint Paul federal trial, and the South St. Paul payroll robbery whose suspected getaway route turned up in a Cleveland notebook all have close local ties and contemporary records.

### Cited Findings

- **The tenants in the detective magazine (West St. Paul, April 1932).** Minnesota Historical Society's MNopedia credits landlord's son Nick Hannegraf with recognizing Alvin Karpis and Fred Barker in *True Detective Mysteries*. The April 30, 1932 Minnesota Bureau of Criminal Apprehension report, Case 1075, identifies the April issue and the historical rental as 1031 South Robert Street. On pp. 4–5, the agents record that Nick identified the two men and had noted the license plates of cars coming and going. He said the departing tenants left the door open, the gas on, the radio playing and part of a cooked meal on the table. On p. 7 the agents report the tenants had taken Mrs. Hannegraf's silverware. The BCA notes a magazine reward of $100 on pp. 5–6. The 1936 FBI Bremer summary, printed p. 6, adds that the landlady had noticed the tenants carrying a violin case. [BCA Case 1075, reproduced in FBI Vault Kansas City Massacre Part 48](https://vault.fbi.gov/Kansas%20City%20Massacre/Kansas%20City%20Massacre%20Part%2048/), [FBI Bremer investigation summary, pp. 5–6](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/), [MNopedia Barker–Karpis Gang](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/barker-karpis-gang).
- **Three escapes and a Saint Paul courtroom (1925–1935).** The FBI's November 1936 case summary, printed pp. 10–11, records that Edna and Jack Murray were convicted of the Kansas City robbery of H. H. Southward in 1925. The press called her the “Kissing Bandit,” corroborated as a period nickname by the October 2, 1925 *Nowata Daily Star* (which prints “Kiss-Me”). The FBI credits Murray with three escapes from Missouri State Penitentiary. On December 13, 1932, Murray and another prisoner, Irene McCann, escaped after sawing through their cell bars. The same investigation records that Murray went to trial at the Federal Courts Building in Saint Paul on April 15, 1935 in proceedings arising from the Bremer kidnapping. On May 6 the charges against her were dismissed. She returned to prison in Missouri to complete the earlier robbery sentence and received two additional years for escaping (printed p. 45). [FBI Bremer investigation summary, pp. 10–11 and 44–46](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/), [Nowata Daily Star, October 2, 1925, p. 1](https://gateway.okhistory.org/ark:/67531/metadc1320191/m1/1/).
- **The payroll robbery and the notebook (South St. Paul, August 30, 1933).** The Swift and Company payroll was robbed outside the South St. Paul Post Office. Leo Pavlak was killed and another officer was wounded. A later police raid in Cleveland turned up a notebook with a getaway chart. An FBI investigation traced its zero point to Stockyards National Bank and connected the chart to the robbery. The 1936 summary expresses that connection as the investigators' conclusion. The 1936 internal memorandum says agents followed the route and identified it as the escape chart for the robbery. [Minnesota House account of the Pavlak commemoration](https://www.house.mn.gov/Caucus/View/DFL/35747), [FBI Bremer investigation summary, printed p. 14](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/), [FBI memorandum for the director, May 12, 1936, p. 5, in the Eliot Ness file](https://vault.fbi.gov/Eliot%20Ness/Eliot%20Ness%20Part%2001/at_download/file).

### Gaps

- The BCA says its office received the magazine information April 27, and the later FBI summary dates the initial report to police April 25. The BCA report gives a different departure sequence from the FBI summary, so use April 1932 and avoid a scene depicting everyone leaving together. The identity of any person who warned the gang is unresolved in this pass. The sources here leave the contents of the violin case and whether anyone claimed the magazine reward unknown. The 1932 West Plains wanted poster is cataloged, but do not pass it off as the magazine issue. [BCA Case 1075](https://vault.fbi.gov/Kansas%20City%20Massacre/Kansas%20City%20Massacre%20Part%2048/), [FBI 1936 summary](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/), [MNHS BCA criminal history files inventory, Reserve 20](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/pubsaf08.html).
- Use the kiss as a nickname or period press claim. The examined sources do not independently establish the gesture, and the newspaper and FBI summary use different ages for Murray, so omit her age. The quoted period wording also varies. The dismissal of federal charges must be paired with the 1925 state robbery conviction rather than telling them as one judgment. [Nowata Daily Star, October 2, 1925, p. 1](https://gateway.okhistory.org/ark:/67531/metadc1320191/m1/1/), [FBI Bremer investigation summary, pp. 10–11 and 45](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/).
- The payroll figure varies across retellings ($30,000 in the FBI summary and $33,000 in Historic Twin Cities). The article need not name an amount or assign who personally fired. I located no scan of the chart itself. Paula Harmon denied ever seeing the notebook and said its writing was not Fred Barker's. [FBI Bremer investigation summary, printed p. 14](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/), [Historic Twin Cities, South St. Paul Post Office](https://www.historictwincities.com/2019/12/06/south-st-paul-post-office/), [FBI Vault Bremer Part 58, p. 58](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%2058).

## Which out of town scenes have a direct Saint Paul connection?

### Takeaway

The gang's attempts to change their fingerprints connect two existing tour strands, and the FBI's account of arresting Karpis with a necktie provides an odd coda to the Hamm kidnapping.

### Cited Findings

- **The men who tried to change their fingerprints (1934).** The FBI's Bremer summary, printed p. 33, says Dr. Joseph Moran performed facial and finger operations on Volney Davis, Harry Campbell and Arthur “Doc” Barker near Toledo, Ohio. It says all three had such pain in their fingers that they had to be fed by James Wilson, Wynona Burdette and Edna Murray, and describes similar operations on Alvin Karpis and Fred Barker in Chicago. The same summary, printed p. 45, says a gasoline can bearing Doc Barker's print was introduced over defense objection in the Saint Paul trial. MNopedia reports a Wisconsin farmer found the discarded can. Independently, a contemporary federal report on Dillinger's death records apparent acid-burn scars on his fingertips and prints successfully taken from the body. The Justice Department exhibit catalog reproduces the actual card on p. 23. [FBI Bremer investigation summary, pp. 33 and 45](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/), [MNopedia Barker–Karpis Gang](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/barker-karpis-gang), [FBI Dillinger Part 1, County Morgue paragraph](https://vault.fbi.gov/John%20Dillinger%20/John%20Dillinger%20Part%2001/), [Justice Department museum exhibit catalog, p. 23](https://www.justice.gov/history/media/1459036/dl?inline=).
- **The FBI improvised with a necktie (New Orleans and Saint Paul, 1936).** The FBI's retrospective says agents brought no handcuffs when they arrested Alvin Karpis in New Orleans on May 1, 1936, so one agent removed his tie and bound Karpis's hands. J. Edgar Hoover also told the necktie story in an interview entered in the Congressional Record in 1972. A contemporary newspaper confirms the arrest and his transport to Saint Paul. On July 27 Karpis pleaded guilty to the Hamm kidnapping and received a life sentence, according to the 1936 investigation summary. [FBI, The Alvin Karpis Capture](https://www.fbi.gov/news/stories/a-byte-out-of-history), [Congressional Record, January 19, 1972, p. 373](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1972-pt1/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1972-pt1-2-2.pdf), [The Daily Mail, May 2, 1936, p. 1](https://newspaperarchive.com/the-daily-mail-may-02-1936-p-1/), [FBI Bremer investigation summary, printed p. 54](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/).
- Two optional leads if more geographic range is wanted. FBI files say that after a suspected Fort Scott bank robbery, Francis Keating, Thomas Holden and Harvey Bailey were arrested at Old Mission Golf Course near Kansas City on July 7, 1932. Alvin Karpis and the Barkers subsequently took quarters at White Bear Lake. The 1935 Garrettsville, Ohio mail train robbery helped federal investigators track Karpis and Harry Campbell, who were already wanted in the Bremer case. The postal inspection service recounts a dynamite threat at the mail car. [FBI Vault Bremer Part 159](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20159/at_download/file), [FBI Bremer investigation summary, printed p. 51](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/at_download/file), [US Postal Inspection Service history](https://www.uspis.gov/about/history-of-uspis/organized-crime-and-illegal-schemes).

### Inferences

- The file's account of excruciating surgery sets up a reader-friendly question about why erasing future prints could never undo earlier evidence. A sidebar can link Hamm's existing silver nitrate section, the separate Bremer gas can and Dillinger's scarred print card. The current data does not discuss the surgeries, the can or the postmortem card. [Current stop content](../../src/data/stops.json).
- Use the necktie as a short coda to the arrest and plea, or as a brief scene about an agency seeking credit. The existing Hamm narration covers the ransom letters and fingerprint evidence, then moves to the arrangement breaking down. [Current stop content](../../src/data/stops.json).

### Gaps

- The FBI’s 1936 reconstruction is evidence of the agency's reported surgical findings, with no independent operating record examined. The present case synopsis refers to three gang members and does not give names. For the detailed version use the contemporary printed p. 33. Don't locate the gas can at Bremer's release point or claim the fingerprint alone proved the case. [FBI gang history](https://www.fbi.gov/history/cases-and-criminals/barker-karpis-gang), [FBI Bremer investigation summary](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/).
- The necktie detail is attributed to the agency and Hoover's own later account. The independently read period article confirms the arrest and the Saint Paul return, while it is silent on the tie. The retellings differ on precisely how Hoover took part. Omit that choreography and keep the credited source visible. [FBI arrest history](https://www.fbi.gov/news/stories/a-byte-out-of-history), [Congressional Record](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1972-pt1/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1972-pt1-2-2.pdf), [The Daily Mail, May 2, 1936, p. 1](https://newspaperarchive.com/the-daily-mail-may-02-1936-p-1/).

# Fugitive paper trails connected to Saint Paul

## Volney Davis, the forced landing and the counterfeit hundred

### Takeaway

A forced landing broke the federal chain of custody in Illinois. Months later, Davis appeared in a Saint Paul courtroom in handcuffs. A separate item in his FBI file explains what he said he meant to do with a counterfeit $100 bill.

### Cited Findings

- The FBI’s November 19, 1936 reconstruction says agents were transporting Davis toward Chicago when their plane made a forced landing near Yorkville, Illinois, in February 1935. It says he escaped amid the confusion. Special Agent Thomas McDade’s contemporaneous diary says the agents took Davis into town to call their office and removed his shackles, after which he knocked one agent down and got away in a car. A government outline of Agent W. F. Trainor’s expected testimony gives a Yorkville hotel as the escape site and dates it February 6, as does McDade. The later FBI summary puts it February 7. [FBI investigation summary, printed p. 43](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/at_download/file#page=49), [Thomas McDade diary transcript, Feb. 6–7 entries](https://socxfbi.org/common/Uploaded%20files/Foundation/MCDADEDiariesFINALTRANSCRIPT-1.pdf), [FBI Vault Part 124, outline of Trainor’s testimony, document p. 4](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20124/)
- The FBI summary reports that a counterfeit $100 was taken from Davis following his first arrest. Davis claimed he kept it to bribe a future arresting officer, reckoning he could get away before anyone spotted the counterfeit. The FBI says he never offered it to its agents. [FBI investigation summary, printed p. 43, paragraph following the account of the forced landing](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/at_download/file#page=49)
- Davis was recaptured in Chicago on June 1. The Associated Press’s June 3 dispatch from Saint Paul reports Davis in handcuffs at his court appearance and records his guilty plea. The later federal ruling records the forced landing and escape, recapture and conspiracy plea in the Saint Paul case. [Roseburg News-Review, June 3, 1935, p. 1, second St. Paul dateline and paragraphs on the court appearance](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/2003260227/1935-06-03/ed-1/seq-1/), [Davis v. United States, 123 F. Supp. 407 (D. Minn. 1954), procedural history](https://app.midpage.ai/document/davis-v-united-states-8719653)

### Inferences

The local scene can open in court with the cuffs, then work back to the Illinois landing. The counterfeit note is a separate object the summary connects to the first arrest. Its intended use is Davis’s explanation.

### Gaps

Use February 1935 in visitor copy or explicitly credit the February 6 date to the diary and Trainor outline. Avoid a specific first-arrest city because the FBI summary is internally inconsistent about St. Louis and Kansas City and the court says Kansas City. McDade describes the escape from an agent’s perspective. The records used here leave the precise sequence inside the hotel and the occupants of the car unclear.

## The Karpis capture that changed directions in ten minutes

### Takeaway

An FBI phone slip records a news service report that Karpis had been captured in the West, followed ten minutes later by an update saying the report came from Saint Paul and the capture was in the East. The bureau’s local office had heard neither.

### Cited Findings

- A “Record of Telephone Call or Visitor” dated April 25, 1935, logs Mr. Suydam of Universal Service at 8:15 p.m. saying Karpis had reportedly been captured in the West. At 8:25 p.m., Suydam called again, attributing the report to Saint Paul and giving the East as the region. The FBI clerk records calling the Saint Paul office, where Mr. Ladd said he had heard nothing about it and would look into it. [FBI Vault Part 129, April 25, 1935 call slip, file stamp 576-5809](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20129/)
- Karpis was taken into federal custody in New Orleans on May 1, 1936. Agent E. J. Connelley’s report records the capture and that questioning continued the next morning at the Saint Paul office. [Contemporary FBI report reproduced in Bremer Kidnapping Part 225, first synopsis page](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/54dc6b0be4b0d364a5ee20e0/54dc6cb3e4b0658f0cbac319/54dc6cb3e4b0658f0cbac31d/1365506018617/EJCKarpisRpt.pdf), [FBI historical overview, final section](https://www.fbi.gov/history/cases-and-criminals/barker-karpis-gang)

### Inferences

The scene can follow the two timestamps on the slip and the call to Ladd. The actual arrest is a closing date to distinguish the two events. The phone slip itself is the new incident. The previous Casebook already covers the New Orleans capture and improvised handcuffs.

### Gaps

The slip records what Suydam said about the rumor’s origin. It does not identify the Saint Paul source or establish that the news service published the rumor.

## The dedicated tip-off telephone Jesse Doyle described

### Takeaway

A federal prison interview records a story about a telephone installed for one caller. Jesse Doyle told an FBI agent that an apartment telephone in Saint Paul was set aside for tips from a state official.

### Cited Findings

- A November 11, 1935 FBI letter records Special Agent G. H. Wilson’s November 7 interview with Jesse Doyle at the Nebraska State Penitentiary. Doyle said he had known Fred Barker since childhood, said he had split away before the Bremer kidnapping, and alleged that the gang reserved a telephone in its Saint Paul apartment for calls from a person in the State Apprehension Bureau at the Capitol. Doyle said that on one occasion nobody was there to answer and a call was routed to Harry Sawyer’s place. [FBI Vault Part 171, Nov. 11, 1935 “Personal and Confidential” letter, document p. 1, file 7-576-8042](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20171/)

### Inferences

The defensible opening is an interview record that describes a silent apartment and a call routed elsewhere. Attribute the allegation from the first sentence. The 1935 document predates and is distinct from the Casebook’s court-documented Cedar 5328 police line and the recordings played to jurors.

### Gaps

The document records Doyle’s account. It does not independently establish the line, the apartment address, the caller’s identity or a particular raid warning. No number or recording supports a precise reconstruction.

## Four pouches and a gunmetal Plymouth

### Takeaway

The Garrettsville mail robbery left investigators a description of the getaway car specific enough to include the key number. It gave postal inspectors and FBI agents a fresh trail to people wanted in the Saint Paul Bremer case.

### Cited Findings

- A Post Office Department circular reproduced in the FBI file says armed robbers held up a train at the Erie station in Garrettsville, Ohio, at 2:14 p.m. on November 7, 1935. Two men entered the mail car. The circular itemizes two registered-mail and two ordinary-mail pouches. The accompanying investigative material describes a gunmetal gray 1935 Plymouth sedan with two windshield wipers, two taillights, a trunk and key no. MP 802. [FBI Vault Part 179, December 16, 1935 inspector interview and paragraph beginning “A circular issued by the Post Office Department”](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20179/at_download/file)
- At that December stage, the inspector said a witness had identified Nelson, alias Coker, and described Karpis and Harry Campbell as suspects. The November 1936 case summary reports that subsequent investigation identified both Karpis and Campbell as participants and explicitly connects their pursuit to the Saint Paul Bremer case. The arrest report says Karpis was taken with Fred Hunter in New Orleans in May and questioning of Karpis continued in Saint Paul. [FBI Vault Part 179, inspector Casey’s identification discussion](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20179/at_download/file), [FBI investigation summary, printed p. 51](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/at_download/file), [FBI report reproduced in Bremer Kidnapping Part 225, first synopsis page](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/54dc6b0be4b0d364a5ee20e0/54dc6cb3e4b0658f0cbac319/54dc6cb3e4b0658f0cbac31d/1365506018617/EJCKarpisRpt.pdf)
- The Postal Inspection Service’s retrospective account says Karpis used a dynamite threat to get the mail clerks to open the doors. The primary circular passage does not describe dynamite. [U.S. Postal Inspection Service history, Garrettsville paragraphs](https://www.uspis.gov/about/history-of-uspis/organized-crime-and-illegal-schemes)

### Inferences

The clearest archival scene is the circular spread out beside the Plymouth description, with the pouches and the key number as anchors. The city connection is the investigators’ pursuit of the Saint Paul kidnapping suspects and later Saint Paul questioning. The robbery occurred in Ohio.

### Gaps

The sources disagree on the train number and initial number of robbers. Omit both. Avoid implying Karpis and Campbell were convicted in the train case or that the Saint Paul interrogation specifically concerned the train. The dynamite detail is optional with retrospective attribution. The prior research listed the robbery as a reserve lead. The postal circular, vehicle and identification chronology are new evidence.

# Saint Paul gang files and overlooked encounters

## Which small events come through clearly in the records?

### Takeaway

The strongest small scenes here are a cop who said he saw a confiscated gun back on the same man two hours later, and a badly wounded airline radio operator whose uniform the gang allegedly mistook for a police uniform. The Van Meter and Eddie Green episodes let witnesses and objects carry the story after the big-name fugitives have left.

### Cited Findings

- **Tommy Carroll walks past with the gun again.** In his 1979 oral history, former Saint Paul police officer Herbert Scott said that he and patrol partner Boyd Carrier had confiscated a .45 automatic from Tommy Carroll on Robert Street and taken him to the detectives. Detectives told the patrolmen to leave him there and that they would do the report. Scott remembered seeing Carroll about two hours later on Robert Street, deliberately opening his coat to show that he had the gun. Scott said Carrier wrote a report despite the instruction and that they later could not find it. [Saint Paul Police Oral History, Herbert Scott, printed pp. 17–18, exchange beginning “This was sometime before when Boyd Carrier and I were working 14”](https://spphs.org/oral_history/histories/Scott_Herb_1979.pdf).
- **Airline uniforms mistaken for police uniforms.** The Bureau’s November 19, 1936 Bremer reconstruction says three uniformed Northwest Airways employees were near Portland Avenue and St. Alban Street on January 13, 1934, after two gang members left a planning meeting. The Bureau says the men thought they were being followed by officers and opened fire, badly wounding McCord, and that Harry Sawyer then insisted on delaying the Bremer kidnapping. A separate January 1935 federal memo says Roy McCord viewed Arthur “Doc” Barker and could not identify him as the gunman. A 2008 Star Tribune interview with Babe Rohland records that McCord was a radio operator, that he recovered, and that her mother had watched events unfold outside their home. [Bremer investigation summary, printed p. 17, paragraph beginning “The gang had originally planned”](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/at_download/file), [Bremer Part 82, printed p. 111, January 1935, find “Roy McCord, who was shot”](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%2082), [Star Tribune, Babe Rohland interview](https://www.startribune.com/babe-rohland-putting-a-face-on-history/18832684).
- **A call about a dog and a girl on her porch.** Former patrolman Herbert Scott recalled responding to a dog hit by a car near University Avenue, hearing a volley to the east, and finding Homer Van Meter in the alley behind a filling station near Marion and University. In a separate childhood memoir, Bernice Fisher recalled standing on her porch at 193 West University and watching police leave squad cars and run up the alley across the street. An Aug. 24, 1934 AP dispatch confirms Van Meter was killed in Saint Paul the previous night. [Scott oral history, pp. 19–20, find “dog hit by a car”](https://spphs.org/oral_history/histories/Scott_Herb_1979.pdf), [Fisher, Ramsey County History, Spring 2003, p. 25, find “I stood on the front porch”](https://rchs.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/RCHS_Spring2003_Fisher.pdf), [Aug. 24, 1934 Medford Mail Tribune, p. 1, AP dispatch beginning “ST. PAUL, Minn., Aug. 24”](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn97071090/1934-08-24/ed-1/seq-1/ocr/).
- **The keys in Eddie Green’s pocket.** A federal memo dated April 10, 1934 says that agents traced associates using the name Stephens to a different Saint Paul apartment on Marshall Avenue after the Lincoln Court shootout. They followed baggage from there and waited for Eugene “Eddie” Green to collect it at a second location. Agents shot him on April 3 and he died in hospital April 10. The memo reports that keys found on Green were ones he said belonged to Dillinger. It also says a gunstock found at the Marshall Avenue apartment appeared to fit a machine gun left behind at Lincoln Court. The Aug. 24 Medford AP dispatch independently acknowledges federal agents fatally wounded Green in Saint Paul. [John Dillinger Gang Part 12, April 10, 1934 memorandum beginning “I have just been advised that Eugene Green,” filing stamp 62-29777-4721](https://vault.fbi.gov/John%20Dillinger%20/john/John%20Dillinger%20Gang%20Part%2012), [Aug. 24, 1934 Medford Mail Tribune, p. 1, Green mentioned in AP report on Van Meter](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn97071090/1934-08-24/ed-1/seq-1/ocr/).
- **The copycats and the dummy ransom.** On June 30, 1932, 20-year-old Haskell Bohn was taken near the rear garage of his family’s home on Summit Avenue. The contemporary AP account describes a demand for $35,000 written in pencil on cheap paper. A July 7 AP dispatch reports his return and says police held three local men who allegedly confessed to a separate attempt to extort $10,000 from his father by promising the young man’s return. Police used a dummy package of money to trap them. A same-day Oklahoma newspaper also reports that police held men suspected of the $10,000 extortion. [Daily Iowan, July 1, 1932, p. 3, “Youth Seized by Kidnapers for Ransom”](https://dailyiowan.lib.uiowa.edu/DI/1932/di1932-07-01.pdf), [Daily Iowan, July 8, 1932, p. 3, “Ransom Not Paid for Bohn”](https://dailyiowan.lib.uiowa.edu/DI/1932/di1932-07-08.pdf), [Evening Derrick, July 7, 1932, p. 1](https://gateway.okhistory.org/ark:/67531/metadc2070228/m1/1/).

### Inferences

- The Carroll account gives the general protection story a concrete firsthand scene with rank-and-file officers, a visible gun and a missing report. It stands independently of Scott’s Van Meter story. If both run, emphasize Fisher’s porch for Van Meter to vary the source and point of view. [Scott oral history, pp. 17–20](https://spphs.org/oral_history/histories/Scott_Herb_1979.pdf), [Fisher memoir](https://rchs.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/RCHS_Spring2003_Fisher.pdf).
- McCord's shooting provides a distinct civilian incident in the Bremer chronology, and Green’s baggage pickup is a separate local encounter days after the existing Lincoln Court stop. The Casebook and stop material checked do not describe Carroll, McCord, the Van Meter alley, Green’s keys or the Bohn copycat trap. ### Gaps

- Carroll’s episode is a first-person recollection recorded decades after the events. No dated arrest record or independent account of the gun being returned was located. Say what Scott recalled rather than assigning an exact date, naming an official who released Carroll, or treating the report as destroyed. [Scott oral history, pp. 17–18](https://spphs.org/oral_history/histories/Scott_Herb_1979.pdf).
- The Bremer summary calls the victim P. N. McCord and the separate memo calls him Roy McCord. The latter name appears in MNopedia and press coverage. The specific shooter remains unidentified by McCord, and motive and the decision to delay are the Bureau’s reconstruction. [Bremer summary, p. 17](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/at_download/file), [Bremer Part 82, p. 111](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%2082), [MNopedia gang timeline](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/barker-karpis-gang).
- Scott misremembers police ranks and the interviewer's street reference differs from Scott’s. Attribute the injured-dog call to Scott and leave its corner approximate. Fisher’s title misclassifies Van Meter as Barker–Karpis, while contemporary AP coverage identifies him as Dillinger’s associate. Neither memory should settle who fired first or why Van Meter was found there. [Scott oral history, p. 19 and footnotes](https://spphs.org/oral_history/histories/Scott_Herb_1979.pdf), [Fisher memoir, p. 25](https://rchs.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/RCHS_Spring2003_Fisher.pdf), [Contemporary AP reporting](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn97071090/1934-08-24/ed-1/seq-1/ocr/).
- The first Green memo says 778 Como Street, while an interview in the same file with the cook Opal has 778 Rondo as the location in a newspaper clipping. Avoid assigning a historical shooting address from the examined paperwork. The stock’s apparent fit and the keys’ ownership are the memo’s report, and the Bureau describes the shooting from its agents' point of view. [Dillinger Gang Part 12, find “I have just been advised” and “Leona lives at 778 Rondo”](https://vault.fbi.gov/John%20Dillinger%20/john/John%20Dillinger%20Gang%20Part%2012).
- The dummy package and alleged confessions come from police statements reported by AP. No disposition for those three men was located. Avoid merging their conduct with the kidnapping or stating a ransom outcome as settled. The contemporary father denied payment, while a 1934 Time account reported $12,000 paid and attributed the actual abduction to Verne Sankey’s confession. [July 8 Daily Iowan, p. 3](https://dailyiowan.lib.uiowa.edu/DI/1932/di1932-07-08.pdf), [Time, “Lindbergh Law and After”](https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0%2C33009%2C882242-1%2C00.html).

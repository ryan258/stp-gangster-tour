# Gang records in Saint Paul

## Who else was on William Hamm’s flight?

### Takeaway

On July 23, 1933, five weeks after the kidnapping, William Hamm Jr. and a man the FBI implicated in his abduction traveled on the same Northwest Airways flight out of Saint Paul. The passenger manifest became evidence in the investigation.

### Cited findings

- A March 16, 1936 FBI memo says Bryan Bolton told agents he flew from Saint Paul toward Chicago with Jack Peifer (written Pfeiffer in the memo) and a woman, and saw Hamm and a federal agent aboard. The memo says the Bureau had a statement from Agent Coulter and Northwest Airways passenger manifests that corroborated Bolton’s account, and that Bolton left the flight at Madison, where the subsequent investigation identified him. Locator: March 16, 1936 memo p. 1, search “manifest records.” [FBI Vault, Bremer Kidnapping Part 206](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20206/at_download/file)
- The FBI’s November 1936 case summary names Bolton among the people it said abducted Hamm and attributes his invitation into the plan to his association with Fred Goetz. In July 1936 the press reported that Bolton had pleaded guilty and that prosecutor Sullivan described him as the principal government witness in the Hamm proceedings. Locators: FBI summary printed pp. 13–14, search “he was invited to participate”; newspaper p. 5, search “principal government witness.” [FBI Vault, Bremer Investigation Summary Part 1](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/at_download/file), [Daily Iowan, July 15, 1936](https://dailyiowan.lib.uiowa.edu/DI/1936/di1936-07-15.pdf)

### Inferences and gaps

The memo combines Bolton’s recollection with the Bureau’s account of corroboration. It gives no established reason Bolton boarded, why he left the plane at Madison, or whether Hamm recognized him or even knew he was aboard. “Left the flight” is the supported action. The original manifests and Agent Coulter’s statement are described in the memo.

## What was a Shell-marked coupe doing in the Bremer ransom handoff?

### Takeaway

Federal investigators reconstructed a handoff involving a Chevrolet coupe with Shell signs, a bus to Rochester and a series of light signals. The route began at 969 University Avenue in Saint Paul.

### Cited findings

- On February 6, 1934, according to the Bureau’s case summary, ransom courier Walter Magee transferred the money into the Shell-marked Chevrolet at 969 University Avenue and found keys and further instructions in the left front door pocket. The instructions led him to follow a Rochester bus from Farmington through Cannon Falls and Zumbrota. He reported seeing four red roadside lights. After he took a gravel road, a car behind him flashed its headlights five times and he left the two suit boxes at the roadside. Locator: printed pp. 23–24 and the top of the following scan, search “five flashes” or “Cannon Falls.” [FBI Vault, Bremer Investigation Summary Part 1](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/at_download/file)
- A field report documents agents pursuing one of the original Shell signs as a physical clue. A sign writer thought it had been printed professionally and investigators contacted the sign painters’ union. See E. N. Notestein’s report at PDF page 38. [FBI Vault, Bremer Kidnapping Part 29](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%2029/at_download/file#page=38)

### Inferences and gaps

The report describes the car’s appearance and what Magee encountered. It does not show Shell’s involvement, who operated the lights or who made the signs. The existing story covers the abduction and demands; the courier’s route and the sign inquiry are distinct episodes.

## How did Larry De Vol’s trail run from Grand Avenue to a fire hose?

### Takeaway

Police traced some of the loot from a fatal Minneapolis bank robbery to a Saint Paul apartment. Its occupant later escaped with a group from the state hospital at St. Peter, where contemporaneous reporting describes a fire hose used to get down from a barred window.

### Cited findings

- The Bureau’s 1936 summary places Larry De Vol in a Grand Avenue apartment in Saint Paul and ties him to the December 16, 1932 robbery at Third Northwestern National Bank in Minneapolis, in which two police officers and a civilian were killed. The summary reports that De Vol pleaded guilty to murder and received a life term at Stillwater. A contemporary wire story identified him under the name Lawrence Barton and attributed to the county attorney a signed statement about planning and sharing in the proceeds, plus the recovery of some loot in his Saint Paul apartment. The same dispatch says Barton refused to admit taking part in the raid. Locators: FBI summary printed p. 10, search “Larry De Vol also was in St. Paul”; newspaper p. 5, “Bank Robbery Suspects Held.” [FBI Vault, Bremer Investigation Summary Part 1](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/at_download/file), [Daily Iowan, December 21, 1932](https://dailyiowan.lib.uiowa.edu/DI/1932/di1932-12-21.pdf)
- On June 8, 1936, the press reported that De Vol helped organize an escape the previous evening from a secure ward at the St. Peter state hospital. A separate newspaper edition reports that the group struck guards with chair legs, pried loose a barred window and used a fire hose to descend. It also places De Vol’s prior incarceration at Stillwater. Locators: La Grande p. 1 and Medford p. 3. [La Grande Evening Observer, June 8, 1936](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/2006260039/1936-06-08/ed-1/seq-1/), [Medford Mail Tribune, June 8, 1936](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn97071090/1936-06-08/ed-1/seq-3/ocr/)

### Inferences and gaps

Attribute the method to contemporary reporting. The reports may share underlying wire coverage and do not establish who wielded a chair leg or personally went down the hose. The Grand Avenue apartment supplies the Saint Paul connection. The bank was in Minneapolis and the escape was in St. Peter.

## What could three six-dollar utility deposits tell the FBI?

### Takeaway

Investigators tracing the Barker–Karpis gang through Saint Paul followed electricity deposits, unclaimed refunds and a White Bear Lake post-office-box lead. Some traces went cold.

### Cited findings

- A March 22, 1934 field report includes inquiries into three Grand Avenue apartments whose Northern States Power deposits stood in the names Charles E. Moore, Edward L. Burns and J. H. Lockwood. The first deposit was $6 and the report describes the other two as similar. Two refund checks were returned unclaimed. The third had not been returned and there was no remaining credit. Agents made tracings of signatures from utility applications. See O. G. Hall’s Form 1, St. Paul file 7-30, stamped 7-576-1384, PDF page 13. [FBI Vault, Bremer Kidnapping Part 29](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%2029/at_download/file#page=13)
- The same report follows rented post-office boxes and records that the White Bear telephone manager found no listings under the names put to him. Investigators suspected a residence in the area around January 11, 1934 but had yet to locate it. See PDF pages 13–14. [FBI Vault, Bremer Kidnapping Part 29](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%2029/at_download/file#page=14)

### Inferences and gaps

These are investigation leads rather than established identities. The utility record does not establish who cashed a check or whether the three names were aliases used by identified gang members. The White Bear Lake lead also cannot be assigned to the separate June 1933 Bald Eagle Lake cottage mentioned in the later FBI case summary. [FBI Vault, Bremer Investigation Summary Part 1](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/at_download/file)

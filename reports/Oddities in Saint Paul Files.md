# The files preserve the odd details

Six new Casebook stories follow what a witness, investigator, or court could check. One appeal records a prosecution expert’s comparison of two pistols. Another puts a bank vault visit beside a freight car’s arrival. In other records, a wedding ends in an arrest, police ask clothing shops to watch for fugitives, and a prosecutor describes thieves who allegedly relocked the mailbags they opened.

## Scrap iron hides barrels and two pistols share a mark

**The whiskey under the scrap iron.** Lift the scrap from three freight entries to see how many whiskey barrels the federal indictment listed beneath it. The ledger then sets a bank vault entry beside the railroad dates. Justice Quinn used the gap to question Michael Weisman’s account of when he paid Hennepin County Attorney William Nash. Open the outcomes from two proceedings. A federal jury acquitted Nash. Governor Burnquist held a removal hearing at the State Capitol in Saint Paul and later removed Nash from office. The Minnesota Supreme Court left that decision in place. [Federal appeal, pp. 213–215](https://openjurist.org/277/f1d/211/goldberg-v-united-states), [Removal opinion and dissent](https://law.counselstack.com/opinion/in-re-mason-minn-1920), [MNHS account of the Capitol hearing, C214–C216](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/winnipeg-liquor-conspiracy)

**The two Colts with aluminum grips.** Start in Como Park, where J. H. Peterson testified he saw a white sack move from a disabled Lincoln to a smaller car. Then compare the pistols found on Leonard Hankins and Larry De Vol. Both had **aluminum grips and chiseled-off serial numbers**. A prosecution expert said the same tool had made the marks. A second comparison sets the Wabasha Street barber’s haircut alibi beside Hankins’s own statement that he was traveling from Superior. The court upheld denial of a new trial. Its opinion leaves the sack’s contents and the identity of the person who altered the pistols open. [Minnesota Supreme Court, pp. 375–382, C217–C219](https://openjurist.org/193/minn/375/state-v-hankins-alias-lewis)

## A Wisconsin wreck leads to a Saint Paul courtroom

**The telephone pole that stopped Touhy.** Follow the timeline from the Elkhorn crash, starting with the court’s account and the newspaper report of the unarmed officer who stopped the men. Compare Touhy’s later recollection that he agreed to pay **$22.50** for the pole with the county history’s account of Willie Sharkey hitching a ride on a chicken truck. The bill itself remains unverified. On July 22 a newspaper published a photo of Touhy in custody. In Saint Paul, a federal jury later acquitted the four men on the Hamm charges. [Kewaskum Statesman](https://kewaskumhistoricalsociety.org/assets/newspapers/1933/kts-1933-09-01.pdf), [Illinois Supreme Court](https://syfert.com/illinois/cases/361-ill-332/the-people-v-touhy), [Touhy’s memoir, ch. 10](https://rogertouhygangsters.blogspot.com/2013/04/the-stolen-years-by-roger-touhy-in.html), [Walworth County history](https://www.co.walworth.wi.us/252/History), [Custody photograph](https://cdn.manchesterhistory.org/News/Manchester%20Evening%20Hearld_1933-07-22.pdf), [Hamm verdict, C220–C222](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn97071090/1933-11-28/ed-1/seq-1/ocr/)

**The wedding and the two calls.** Start with the arrest memorandum, which places Bruno Austin’s arrest at the end of his Chicago wedding, and check the name **Whitey** in the Saint Paul indictment. Then open each call entry and the FBI office’s reply. Mrs. Austin said her mother answered both calls. The agent said neither came from the Bureau. The callers’ identities and motives remain unknown. The final cards record the Saint Paul court’s order releasing Austin from custody and a later conviction in a separate Chicago murder case. [Wedding memo, pp. 1–2](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20123/at_download/file), [Mrs. Austin’s interview, p. 88](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20112/at_download/file), [Federal summary, C223–C225](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/at_download/file)

## A clothing-store alert and two sets of keys

**The manhunt at the clothing counter.** Read the clothing-store alert after Alvin Karpis and Harry Campbell fled an Atlantic City hotel. Two days after the escape, a Saint Paul indictment named both men in the Bremer case. The Associated Press put both men in slippers and overcoats and quoted authorities who believed they were still on the island. Compare the alert with the later Bureau route through Pennsylvania, where the Bureau reported that they abandoned a stolen Pontiac near Quakertown. The records leave the timing of the bridge patrols, the route off the island, and any visit to a clothing store unresolved. [Clothing alert, p. 1](https://newspapers.swco.ttu.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/46d5eb78-5306-4ddf-a2c4-184ad817d6b3/content), [Federal summary, C226–C228](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/at_download/file)

**The mailbags they locked again.** Open cards for the mailroom lock and the mailbag lock in the order a federal prosecutor described to the United Press. He alleged that Jack Segar brought two sets of keys to Saint Paul, recruited a crew and used the keys to steal registered mail. The crew allegedly locked both the sacks and the building again. The dispatch traces the investigation to a **$100 Liberty bond** and lists the arrests. It leaves the outcome for the men open. [United Press, p. 1](https://gateway.okhistory.org/ark:/67531/metadc1524843/m1/1/), [Mailroom account, p. 5, C229–C231](https://gateway.okhistory.org/ark:/67531/metadc1524843/m1/5/)

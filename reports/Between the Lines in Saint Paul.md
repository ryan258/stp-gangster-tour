# Six records change the story

A Ramsey County deputy told federal agents he checked into a Saint Paul hotel as **Ben Franklin**. A newspaper declared a bootlegger dead twelve days before he was shot near Snelling and University. In six new Casebook stories, readers can open the deputy’s expense request, follow the security arrangements for a kidnapping trial, trace a typewriter through federal offices, and compare accounts of what happened. The documents preserve details that seldom make the retellings, including the point where an investigator had to stop and ask for more evidence.

## A guard checks in and two judges go downstairs

**Ben Franklin at the Frederic.** Open the June 1935 letter about the notes special deputy Jack Hession carried between Arthur “Doc” Barker and Mildred Kuhlman. Sheriff Thomas Gibbons arranged for federal agents to receive copies before delivery, according to the letter. Unfold Hession’s account of signing in as Ben Franklin, then open his **$26.50** expense request for the room and telephone charges. Hession expected Barker to pay. After Barker was moved to Leavenworth, Saint Paul asked Washington to cover the bill. The surviving letter leaves the payment unsettled. [Andersen’s June 16 letter, pp. 1–2, C156–C158](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20140)

**The judges went downstairs.** Follow the four Touhy defendants from a special car on a Milwaukee mail train to a motions hearing in the Ramsey County jail basement. The Associated Press reported that machine guns guarded the hearing. When the trial opened, deputies had tear gas guns in the courtroom. The sequence ends with the November 28 verdict. The jury acquitted all four men on the **two federal Hamm charges**. [Train dispatch, p. 6](https://dailyiowan.lib.uiowa.edu/DI/1933/di1933-08-20.pdf), [Hearing dispatch, p. 3](https://dailyiowan.lib.uiowa.edu/DI/1933/di1933-09-26.pdf), [Trial opening, p. 1](https://texashistory.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metapth1394768/m1/1/), [Verdict, p. 1, C159–C161](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn97071090/1933-11-28/ed-1/seq-1/ocr/)

## A purple ribbon and a premature headline

**The typewriter on the supply shelf.** Open the three lines federal agents wanted typed on paper like the Bremer ransom notes, including the one about the quick brown fox. Compare two leads from roughly **500 Saint Paul repair slips**, including a portable Corona that once used a smudgy purple ribbon. Then follow a recovered Underwood to a Bureau supply shelf. The October letter says its laboratory routing labels were missing. The proposed fingerprint check and any match to the ransom notes remain unresolved. [February 26 memo](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%2017), [Repair slips, before stamp 320](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/barker-karpis/Barker%20Karpis%20Part%2077), [Supply-room correspondence, stamps 239–241, C162–C164](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%2059/)

**The headline that buried Abe Wagner early.** Jump from the July 13, 1932 dispatch relaying the *New York Sun*’s claim about a barrel of cement in the East River to July 25, when Anne E. Cowie places the shooting in Saint Paul. Cowie describes Wagner posing as a fruit peddler and records two life sentences. A 1933 federal letter also names the victim as Abe Wagner. It gives different names for the arrested men. The timeline lets readers inspect both sets of names. The exact alias matches remain open. [July 13 dispatch, p. 2](https://cdn.manchesterhistory.org/News/Manchester%20Evening%20Hearld_1932-07-13.pdf#page=2), [County history, p. 12](https://rchs.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/RCHS_Fall2000_Cowie.pdf#page=11), [Cullen letter, p. 2, C165–C167](https://vault.fbi.gov/Kansas%20City%20Massacre/Kansas%20City%20Massacre%20Part%2011/)

## A ransom dispute and a hallway cover story

**The dress shop and the Silver Slipper.** Set the October 1931 dispatch describing Rutman as a dress shop proprietor beside Cowie’s account naming him as a bootlegger held at a Mahtomedi gambling spot. Then open the three statements about the ransom. Rutman said his captors demanded **$50,000**. The police chief denied any payment, and friends said they had heard that relatives paid. Whether money changed hands remains unresolved. [October 8 dispatch, p. 1](https://newspapers.swco.ttu.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/9b93ceed-2dfb-4fb1-972a-4afcf815f9ee/content), [County history, p. 12](https://rchs.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/RCHS_Fall2000_Cowie.pdf#page=11), [Longer ransom dispatch, C168–C170](https://newspapers.swco.ttu.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/14e6a650-8275-481e-b08f-539ddb75bdb3/content)

**The man who said he sold soap.** Compare the April 8 memo about the man in the Lincoln Court hallway with Bessie Skinner’s interview. Agents noted a resemblance between Homer Van Meter and Eddie Green in photographs. They had found no Van Meter fingerprints in the material examined at that point. Skinner said Van Meter later told her he had used the soap story and fired at an officer. The FBI’s later history identifies Van Meter, while the soap claim comes from the case file. [April 8 memo and Skinner interview, p. 10](https://vault.fbi.gov/John%20Dillinger%20/john/John%20Dillinger%20Gang%20Part%2012), [FBI case history, C171–C173](https://www.fbi.gov/history/cases-and-criminals/john-dillinger)

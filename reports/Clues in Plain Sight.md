# Ordinary things turn up in evidence

Seven new Casebook stories let readers inspect a Buick’s forgotten brochures, follow goggles from a Rochester roadside to a federal evidence list, and unfold ransom directions hidden inside a cornmeal box. The details come from court-related correspondence, agents’ reports, and newspaper accounts. Each interaction follows what somebody in the record could see, read, or check. A remembered object sometimes led investigators to something they could recover. Sometimes all that survived was the account of someone who said they saw it.

## A streetcar sighting and a city in mourning

**The Buick spotted from a streetcar.** Follow the owner’s sighting near University Avenue and Dale Street, then look inside the Buick for brochures from his own nut-shelling plant. Historian Paul R. Gold says the owner called police and Sam Taran appeared in court on a car theft charge. Gold records Taran’s release and quotes the *St. Paul Daily News* reporting the judge’s advice to stick to tailoring. The release is the last step documented in this account. [Gold, “The Car Thief,” printed p. 14, C174–C176](https://rchs.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/RCHS_Winter1998_Gold.pdf#page=4)

**The hospital crowd and Hogan’s Christmas turkeys.** Turn through dispatches from the hospital to the funeral. The Associated Press reported that Danny Hogan died after surgery. Curt Brown’s later account describes people coming forward to offer blood and quotes the *Minneapolis Morning Tribune* predicting fewer turkey dinners that Christmas. The identities of donors and any completed transfusions remain unconfirmed. A later history records **five carloads of flowers** and attributes the recollection of Granada Club regulars at the funeral to Harry Turgeon. [AP death report, front page](https://dailyiowan.lib.uiowa.edu/DI/1928/di1928-12-05.pdf), [Brown’s account](https://www.startribune.com/1928-car-bombing-that-killed-st-paul-mob-boss-dapper-dan-hogan-still-unsolved/481153181), [Calvary Cemetery registration, p. 35 and note 45](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/CLG%20%26%20SRB_Calvary%20Cemetery_nomination_tcm36-768759.pdf), [Gold, printed p. 9, C177–C179](https://rchs.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/RCHS_Winter1997_Gold.pdf)

## A ride, a coat, and a pair of goggles

**A two-dollar delivery.** Step through the case hearing. Open the two notes a taxi driver carried to Hamm Brewing manager William Dunn’s home. The driver said he had earned **$2** for the delivery and did not know their contents. Compare the June wire report about his reaction to a photograph of Verne Sankey with an archive description of Leo J. Allison’s courtroom response about Eddie McFadden. Each account describes a tentative reaction. The final scene records the acquittal of all four defendants on the federal Hamm charges. [June 17 dispatch, p. 1](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn90051770/1933-06-17/ed-1/seq-1.pdf), [Kent State photo catalog, item 97](https://www.library.kent.edu/special-collections-and-archives/crime-photographs-collection), [November 28 verdict, p. 1, C180–C182](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn97071090/1933-11-28/ed-1/seq-1/ocr/)

**The coat left in 303.** Compare Patricia Cherrington’s account of a restaurant stop and four days at Harry Sawyer’s cottage with Bessie Skinner’s account of what she heard from Van Meter. Skinner supplied a restaurant name and a Ford sedan, and described the farm destination as her belief. Then unfold the inventory from Lincoln Court Apartment 303 and inspect the coat’s Young-Quinlan label and platinum fox collar. Its owner remains unknown. [Hanni witness outline, after printed p. 5](https://vault.fbi.gov/John%20Dillinger%20/john/John%20Dillinger%20Gang%20Part%2012), [Cherrington account, opening page](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20145), [Skinner interview, C183–C185](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20175)

**The goggles in the snow.** Start with two accounts of the count Bremer was told to make before uncovering his eyes. One says **fifteen**, the other **ten**. Both place the discarded goggles in the weeds. Trace the search behind a Rochester building, where Agent M. P. Glynn’s witness outline records finding them under about three inches of snow, then follow the object to the Bureau’s list of items intended for the Saint Paul U.S. attorney. The exhibit ruling remains unknown. [Witness outline, p. 018 and stamp 649](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20121), [Glynn outline, p. 059](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20122), [Trial summary, p. 5, C186–C188](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20138)

## Two envelopes and a letter inside a box

**The ransom money under the hatband.** Follow the serial numbers a cashier checked against the Bremer ransom list, then inspect the gray felt hat Jack McLaughlin tried on and the brown felt hat where agents reported finding two envelopes. Federal accounts give different cash totals and agree that some bills matched. Open William Vidler’s petition for **$460** and the correspondence about who could reopen the sealed cash. Its return remains unresolved in those letters. A Saint Paul jury acquitted Vidler in the criminal case. [Bank and hat reports, first sheet of the April 30 memo and field pp. 48–49](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%2043/at_download/file), [Petition and correspondence, stamps 186–188 and 233–235](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%2054/at_download/file), [Saint Paul verdict, p. 45, C189–C191](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/at_download/file)

**The ransom note in the cornmeal box.** Open the unclaimed package from a Faribault hotel and unfold directions to a black Chevrolet in Owatonna with a **white dot on its spare tire cover**. The witness outlines record what the messenger said about a sick father and what the note instructed its reader to do. They leave the messenger’s identity and any attempted handoff unresolved. George M. Murray said he forwarded the note to Saint Paul after Bremer’s release. The Bureau listed the box for transfer to the U.S. attorney. [Murray witness outlines, pp. 043–045, C192–C194](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20121)

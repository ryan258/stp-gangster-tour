# Six old records open new stories

A Hudson trade-in came with a request for black wire wheels. A lab examiner recorded a collar button’s diameter to four decimal places. A postal inspector supplied the number of a Plymouth’s key. These details anchor six new Casebook stories drawn from court opinions, trial reporting, and investigative files. Each story has three scenes and links to the underlying claims. [Hudson interviews, S76](https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/fbifiles/gangsterera/johndillinger/dillinger%20gang%20section%2010.pdf), [Bremer clothing report, S80](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/barker-karpis/Barker%20Karpis%20Part%2074), [Postal circular and interviews, S84](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20179/at_download/file).

## A trade-in and a protection payment have competing accounts

**Dillinger’s Hudson and the black wire wheels.** Saint Paul dealer W. J. Potthoff told investigators Irving Gleeman accepted **$331** for his Chevrolet and wanted black wire wheels on the new Hudson. Gleeman recalled holding out for **$375**. An April 2 interview reports $375 on the March 29 transfer agreement. The file describes the agreement without reproducing it. Gleeman explained the $44 difference through a **$206 check credited as $250**. Investigators identified the Hudson as the car Dillinger had when he escaped from Lincoln Court. The Casebook puts the interviews side by side and opens the accounting separately. [C120–C122, S76, printed pp. 16–20 and passages immediately before pp. 21 and 50](https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/fbifiles/gangsterera/johndillinger/dillinger%20gang%20section%2010.pdf), [S11, FBI case history](https://www.fbi.gov/history/cases-and-criminals/john-dillinger).

**The price of police protection.** Ida Dorsey opened a brothel at 151 South Washington Street in 1913. Historian Penny Petersen recounts Nina Clifford testifying that Dorsey paid her **$4,000**, with $1,000 for the house and $3,000 intended for protection. A 1914 dispatch separately reported the bribery convictions of former police chief Martin Flanagan and former chief of detectives Fred Turner. It describes a **$300** charge and leaves the payer unnamed. [C123–C125, S77, MNopedia biography](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/person/dorsey-ida-1866-1918), [S78, Petersen, pp. 167, 213](https://dokumen.pub/minneapolis-madams-the-lost-history-of-prostitution-on-the-riverfront-9780816665235-9780816665242-2013010354-0816665230-9780816665242.html), [S79, 1914 dispatch, p. 2](https://leofrank.org/library/atlanta-constitution-issues/1914/atlanta-constitution-february-21-1914-saturday-14-pages-combined.pdf#page=2).

## A lab examination leads to a Windom shop

**The shirt with no label.** A federal examiner recorded old label stitch holes, a blue crayon number, and a collar button **0.3989 inches across** on a shirt the kidnappers gave Edward Bremer. Saint Paul agents listed two possible retailers. At the Windom shop, Gustave Mueller described a lighter shirt in a different style, a different underwear brand, and socks he did not carry. The shopkeeper’s replies narrowed the search. The buyer and store remain unresolved. [C126–C128, S80, lab report 360, pp. 121–122](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/barker-karpis/Barker%20Karpis%20Part%2074), [S81, February 27 field report, p. 128](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%2017), [S82, March 5 Mueller interview](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/barker-karpis/Barker%20Karpis%20Part%2077).

## A lease, a mail train, and a locked beer parlor

**A new lease, the same bar.** The parties stipulated to four liquor sales at **905 West Seventh** in 1925. A new tenant carried on a soft drink and food business with the same fittings. The appeals court upheld a **one-year closure** in a civil action concerning the property. [C129–C131, S83, *Engler v. United States*, pp. 38–39](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/25/37/1548515/).

**Four mail pouches and a Plymouth key.** A postal circular lists two pouches of registered mail and two of ordinary mail taken in the 1935 Garrettsville, Ohio, robbery. An inspector described a gray Plymouth down to **key No. MP 802**. The FBI later identified Bremer suspects Alvin Karpis and Harry Campbell as participants. How investigators used the key number and the outcome of any train robbery charges remain unresolved. [C132–C134, S84, postal circular and December 16 inspector interview](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20179/at_download/file), [S10, Bremer summary, p. 51](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/at_download/file).

**Seven glasses after closing.** Officers arrived at a licensed beer parlor at **3:15 a.m.** and reported seven partly filled glasses in front of two people and four men hiding in the basement. The owners said they were planning a surprise. Minnesota’s Supreme Court affirmed the after-hours convictions. This and the West Seventh case document local vice enforcement, with no established mob connection in either opinion. [C135–C137, S85, *City of St. Paul v. St. Aubin*, pp. 208–210](https://law.justia.com/cases/minnesota/supreme-court/1937/201-minn-208.html).

## Two leads remain in the notes

Jesse Doyle’s prison interview is the sole account here of the alleged apartment phone reserved for tips. The caller remains unidentified. The Freiermuth photograph belongs to a raid near Hampton in Dakota County. A later family account describes a posed photograph, while the syndicated caption describes an armed encounter. The image itself cannot establish what happened before the pose. [November 11, 1935 Doyle interview letter, pp. 1–2](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20171/), [1924 photograph and caption](https://gateway.okhistory.org/ark:/67531/metadc1599493/m1/7/), [WCCO’s 2024 Hampton account](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/minnesota-farm-at-the-center-of-american-history/).

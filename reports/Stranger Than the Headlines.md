# Strange details survive in Saint Paul

A wire report describes smoke coming from a getaway sedan outside a South Saint Paul post office. At Saint Paul police headquarters, a device adapted from a fire alarm recorder reportedly kept track of which numbers officers dialed. Six new Casebook stories follow what these and other records preserve. The browser interactions let readers inspect an equipment inventory, compare conflicting accounts of an extradition, and open a playbill for Billie Frechette’s act after prison.

## A getaway draws attention and the police station listens

**The Hudson that made its own smoke.** Start outside the South Saint Paul post office, where an Associated Press account describes a sedan arriving with its siren on and smoke coming from the car. Officer Leo Pavlak was killed and Officer John Yeaman was wounded. Open equipment cards for the siren, armor, driver’s door glass, and smoke apparatus that investigators found on a black Hudson abandoned after a Chicago mail robbery. Then compare the mounted Florida plates with the **Duluth Ford plates** found inside and linked by police to the payroll robbery. The federal report gives an inventory and leaves the apparatus’s layout and operation open. [AP reconstruction](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20108), [Sullivan report, pp. 9–12, C195–C197](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20149/at_download/file)

**The lamps at police headquarters.** At the evidence table, open cards for the lamp, telephone, and furniture where period accounts place hidden microphones. Then compare the two recorders. One cut conversations into **aluminum discs**. The other used an adapted fire alarm recorder to log dialed digits on tape. *Modern Mechanix* says a janitor found a recorder in a ventilation shaft. Anne E. Cowie links Jamie’s work to a 1935 grand jury inquiry and twenty-one indictments. The magazine describes the machinery and its discovery. The original tapes and recordings remain unverified. [Vernonia Eagle, p. 2](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn99063854/1935-08-16/ed-1/seq-2/), [Modern Mechanix, pp. 53 and 127](https://electronicsandbooks.com/edt/manual/Old%20Pulp%20Magazines/%21%20new/Modern%20Mechanix.%20-%20July%201936.pdf), [Cowie, p. 13, C198–C200](https://rchs.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/RCHS_Fall2000_Cowie.pdf#page=12)

## Two accounts of a failed transfer

**The Phantom Gunman in Saint Paul.** Compare a California cashier’s identification of Morrie Moll with two accounts of the extradition that followed. The Associated Press put the identification in Saint Paul and identified Moll by his alias, Morrie Miller, a name that appears in the Gleeman brothers’ account of a local liquor syndicate. Later writer J. H. Graham placed the encounter in the governor’s office. The 1929 wire quoted Los Angeles District Attorney Buron Fitts saying the governor refused extradition. Graham says it was approved and Moll disappeared after release on bond. The warrant remains unverified. After the cashier, David Antink, was killed, police sought Moll. An August 1933 dispatch said the people who killed Antink had yet to be apprehended. [Gleeman opinion](https://openjurist.org/170/minn/197/state-v-gleeman), [Identification and police search](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn96088108/1929-09-27/ed-1/seq-1/), [Warrant dispatch](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn96088460/1929-09-27/ed-1/seq-1.pdf), [Graham’s account](https://jhgraham.com/2016/11/10/the-pajama-kid-the-phantom-gunman-and-a-shooting-at-the-bella-napoli/), [1933 follow-up, C201–C203](https://newspapers.swco.ttu.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/a1bbcd8e-5a04-4762-8463-847f0debbd52/content)

## A bond turns up after the search

**The bond in the food can.** Follow Jess Doyle from Saint Paul’s Twin Oaks apartments to Warner Plaza in Kansas City. A federal telegram says agents skipped the food containers in the refrigerator and relays a newspaper account that a janitor found a bond in a can. Trace the **$1,000 Peabody bond, serial number 56**, to the Bank of Drexel, whose cashier identified it as one taken from his bank. The later letter confirms recovery at the apartment. The can and the janitor remain details from the press account. The Saint Paul conspiracy charge against Doyle was dismissed. [Warner Plaza report](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20128/), [Federal summary, pp. 44–45](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/at_download/file), [March 20 telegram](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20108), [Conroy letter, p. 67, C204–C206](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20157/at_download/file)

## An alligator enters the search

**The alligator on the Lake Weir trail.** Follow the map from a Georgia pecan ranch to Chicago and then to Lake Weir, Florida. Bolton told investigators he overheard how men baited a barrel with a small pig to catch a Florida alligator called **Big Joe**. In the story he heard, Big Joe got the bait and escaped the trap. Agents showed him the marked maps they had recovered in Chicago. They searched the Ocala area and located the cottage occupied by Fred and Kate Barker. A later Bureau summary calls the alligator **Old Joe**. The documents place the ranch only roughly and credit the map and the alligator report as leads. [Bolton interview under the name Monte Carter, before p. 105](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%2083), [Bolton outline, p. 110](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20122), [February 8 memorandum, pp. 1–2](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20108), [Federal summary, pp. 38–39, C207–C209](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/at_download/file)

## Billie Frechette takes questions

**Billie Frechette, in person.** Open the playbill at Frechette’s 1936 carnival booking after she served her sentence in the Saint Paul case. The cast cards for *Crime Does Not Pay* follow *The Billboard*’s description of a group photograph that included **unnamed wax figures**. A South Carolina correspondent wrote to Hoover suspecting someone was using her name. In Pittsburgh, an FBI stenographer recognized her from photographs and the field office summarized several of her answers to the audience. Open the audience cards to read the topics in the memo. The Pittsburgh identification leaves the South Carolina appearance unresolved.

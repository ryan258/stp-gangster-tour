# Six odd clues open old files

The Casebook adds six browser stories from Saint Paul’s gangster era. Their clues range from a telegram fragment found under a carpet to a court bill that charged a convicted bootlegger for treating jurors. Some records preserve a witness’s memory or an official’s theory. Others show the decision that followed. Each story has three scenes, with links to the passages behind them.

## A telegram turns up and a court bill comes due

**The telegram under the carpet.** Turn over the photograph notes, trace an Iowa bank to Saint Paul, and open the scrap building manager Daisy Coffey said she found under the carpet. Federal agents recorded a pencil caption that identified the boy in a cherry tree as **John Dillinger at about ten or eleven**. They suspected a getaway purpose for the road chart and sent it to their laboratory. The surviving telegram words leave its sender, recipient, and the meaning of “three” unresolved. [Federal investigation, Saint Paul file 26-2434, printed pp. 10–11 and the passage before p. 16, C138–C140](https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/fbifiles/gangsterera/johndillinger/dillinger%20gang%20section%2010.pdf)

**Gleckman and the jury’s doctor bill.** Inspect the **$6,193.30** bill and open the disputed entries for juror meals, lodging, and a doctor’s services. A separate line bills for an investigator who attended without testifying. The appeals court upheld Leon Gleckman’s tax convictions and ordered the lower court to remove the cost of providing a judge and jury and recalculate the investigator’s expenses. The opinion gives a combined figure for the disputed tribunal costs. The doctor’s individual charge and the final total remain unknown. [*Gleckman v. United States*, pp. 402–404, C141–C143](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/80/394/1503194/)

## Investigators follow a siren and a gold pendant

**The whistle kept going.** Open the written sound sequence. Bremer recalled a factory whistle already blowing when a siren swelled, then the whistle continuing after the siren faded. The Saint Paul office wrote to towns looking for matches. One pastor told agents his church bell tolled **48 times** to mark a parishioner’s death and age. The final scene pairs the request to destroy the circular letters with Hoover’s permission. What was destroyed and whether the sound inquiry located the hideout remain unresolved. [Saint Paul letter, pp. 305–306](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%2048/), [Brookfield interview, before p. 130](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%2025/at_download/file), [April 29 and May 7 letters, C144–C146](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20128/)

**The gold basketball at Wildwood.** Follow the burning headlights near White Bear Lake, inspect **K. C. ’20** on the reported pendant, then compare what two wire services could say about the three men who died. United Press described the token as one of the items police used to identify Frank Coleman. The third man’s identification and a police theory about slot machine concessions were provisional. [United Press, August 14, 1930, pp. 1–2](https://newspapers.swco.ttu.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/79b8eb67-8519-4b74-ac08-1827772b55c8/content), [Associated Press, p. 1, C147–C149](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn96088108/1930-08-14/ed-1/seq-1/)

## A mover’s note and a prosecutor’s inference

**The dishes they left on Grand Avenue.** Follow the caretaker from the laundry room’s squeaking door to the penciled note a mover brought for the dishes and silverware. Then compare three utility refunds. **Two checks came back unclaimed.** The file leaves the third check’s recipient and the occupants’ identities unsettled. [Parnell interviews, stamps 323–330](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/barker-karpis/Barker%20Karpis%20Part%2077), [Utility memo, PDF p. 13, C150–C152](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%2029/at_download/file#page=13)

**The Babe Ruth picture in Sankey’s file.** Turn through three dispatches covering Haskell Bohn’s Saint Paul testimony, the prosecutor’s account of an earnings file on **Babe Ruth and Jack Dempsey**, and Verne Sankey’s arrest in a Chicago barber shop. Open the comparison between what Ramsey County Attorney M. F. Kinkead described and what he inferred. The athletes story remains Kinkead’s allegation. Federal investigator Melvin Purvis attributed admissions to Sankey in the Bohn and Denver cases. [June 1933 testimony](https://dailyiowan.lib.uiowa.edu/DI/1933/di1933-06-13.pdf), [January 3, 1934 dispatch](https://cdn.manchesterhistory.org/News/Manchester%20Evening%20Hearld_1934-01-03.pdf), [February 1 arrest report, C153–C155](https://ecode360.com/LO1625/document/430361415.pdf)

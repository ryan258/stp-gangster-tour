# Seven records open Saint Paul’s underworld

A five-cent cigar check could buy five cents in tobacco or be redeemed for half as much in cash. An FBI call slip put Alvin Karpis’s reported arrest in the West, then in the East ten minutes later. These are two of seven incidents selected for the Casebook’s Paper Trails, where visitors can compare the check’s two values, open entries in the call log, and follow other cases through letters, freight paperwork, and court findings. The scenes present the details the records preserve, with links to the underlying claims and a featured source on each story page. [Whittier to Wolfer, July 18, 1901, files 699–700](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/gr00206/pdf/gr00206-000003-2.pdf), [FBI call slip, April 25, 1935, file stamp 576-5809](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20129/)

## A cigar check and a salesman trade on the Younger name

**Five cents at the cigar counter.** In July 1901, a Saint Paul cigar-shop owner on Wabasha near Seventh proposed paying the newly paroled Cole and Jim Younger **$75 each a month** for a year. Prison agent F. A. Whittier investigated reports of card play and described how the shop redeemed its checks. At the browser counter, choose between **5¢ in cigars or tobacco** and **2½¢ in cash** for one check. Whittier’s letter documents the offer and the proprietor’s explanation. Jim’s later job at Andrew Schoch Grocery’s cigar counter has separate support in an advertising card and Cole’s memoir. [Whittier letter, July 18, both sheets, files 699–700](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/gr00206/pdf/gr00206-000003-2.pdf), [Pardon Board files 633–634, Globe Hotel advertising card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/gr00277.html), [Cole Younger, “On Parole,” chapter 34](https://www.ncows.com/library/pdf/the%20Story%20of%20Cole%20Younger%2C%20by%20Himself.pdf)

**The salesman using Younger’s name.** Read the warden’s August 16 letter beside the Saint Paul granite company’s August 19 reply. The warden said he had heard from two sources that a traveling salesman named Bloom was passing himself off as Cole Younger. P. N. Peterson wrote that he had told Bloom to stop. The prison stamped the reply received **August 20**. The letters preserve the complaint and Peterson’s response. Bloom’s own account remains unknown. [Letters of August 16 and 19, 1901, files 699–700, locate the prison receipt stamp](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/gr00206/pdf/gr00206-000003-2.pdf)

## Freight and a refrigerator leave different paper trails

**The special-delivery envelope.** Follow the stages from Sol Rosenhause’s stopped alcohol truck to a shipment of “Aleo Wash” that reached Saint Paul days later, then to the appeals court. Agents recovered two bills of lading, an account book, a note on Leon Gleckman’s real-estate letterhead, and an envelope addressed to Gleckman at **306 Globe Building**. The court inferred that Gleckman passed the envelope to Rosenhause and affirmed the judgment after Gleckman sought to withdraw his guilty plea. [*Gleckman v. United States*, 16 F.2d 670, printed pp. 674–676, ¶¶ 27–35 and 51](https://openjurist.org/16/f2d/670/gleckman-v-united-states)

**The portable refrigerator.** The inventor’s pitch lays out the people Albert A. Robbins hoped would use his refrigerator, including hunters, motorists, and picnickers, then follows the plans to the pardon board. A 1936 United Press dispatch reported that Robbins submitted them in connection with a request for commutation while serving a 25-year term for his role in Gleckman’s kidnapping. The design and board’s decision remain unlocated. The two surviving newspaper copies cited here carry the same dispatch. [*Ranger Times*, July 16, 1936, p. 3, “Kidnaper In Prison Works On Invention”](https://newspapers.swco.ttu.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/108394a5-9858-4a3d-a4fd-c691ed4b7a5a/content), [*Eastland Telegram*, July 14, 1936, p. 4](https://newspapers.swco.ttu.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/96888fcf-b636-4559-a7fa-67c7c3f59c12/content)

## The federal files preserve an escape and a rumor

**The forced landing.** Advance from the February 1935 landing near Yorkville, Illinois, to the separate account of a counterfeit **$100 bill**, then to Volney Davis’s handcuffed Saint Paul court appearance and guilty plea. Davis told agents he had kept the bill as a prospective bribe. The FBI said he never offered it to them. Its later summary places the escape on February 7, a day after the date in Agent Thomas McDade’s diary and the outline of Agent W. F. Trainor’s expected testimony. [FBI investigation summary, printed p. 43](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/at_download/file#page=49), [McDade diary, February 6–7 entries](https://socxfbi.org/common/Uploaded%20files/Foundation/MCDADEDiariesFINALTRANSCRIPT-1.pdf), [Trainor outline, Part 124, document p. 4](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20124/), [Associated Press, June 3, 1935, p. 1, second Saint Paul dateline](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/2003260227/1935-06-03/ed-1/seq-1/)

**The arrest that changed directions.** Open the **8:15 p.m.** and **8:25 p.m.** entries to see a news-service caller switch the purported Karpis capture from West to East and attribute the report to Saint Paul. The local FBI office had heard nothing. The call slip leaves the original source unidentified. A final entry shows the documented arrest on May 1, 1936, in New Orleans. [FBI Vault Part 129, April 25, 1935, file stamp 576-5809](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20129/), [Agent E. J. Connelley’s report, Part 225, first synopsis page](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/54dc6b0be4b0d364a5ee20e0/54dc6cb3e4b0658f0cbac319/54dc6cb3e4b0658f0cbac31d/1365506018617/EJCKarpisRpt.pdf)

## A vest-pocket book gives way to boxcars

**Tommy Banks’s pocket ledger.** Open the entries for daily liquor sales, his **1929 Saint Paul conspiracy plea and $2,500 fine**, and the later operation run out of Minneapolis’s West Hotel. A 1961 Tax Court opinion records that Banks entered sales in a small book without recording alcohol costs. The later operation ordered boxcars carrying about 80 to 100 drums and kept no books, according to the court’s findings. [*Banks v. Commissioner*, 20 T.C.M. 1165, ¶¶ 20–29, especially ¶¶ 23–29](https://openjurist.org/20/tcm/1165/banks-v-commissioner-4814361)

## Further leads in the files

Two gang-related episodes remain research leads outside the browser collection. Jesse Doyle’s November 1935 prison interview alleges that the gang reserved a Saint Paul apartment telephone for tips from a state official. An Ohio postal robbery circular lists four mail pouches, and the accompanying getaway-car description gives Plymouth key **MP 802**. Investigators linked the Ohio case to the pursuit of Saint Paul’s Bremer kidnapping suspects. [FBI Vault Part 171, November 11 letter, document p. 1, file 7-576-8042](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20171/), [Part 179, December 16 inspector interview and circular](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-kidnapping/Bremer%20Kidnapping%20Part%20179/at_download/file), [FBI investigation summary, printed p. 51](https://vault.fbi.gov/barker-karpis-gang/bremer-investigation-summary/Bremer%20Investigation%20Summary%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/at_download/file)

Three other records concern local vice enforcement, with no established gang link. A combined arrest and search warrant survived review in a 1903 illicit liquor case, but the prosecutor’s comments on the defendant’s silence forced a new trial. A telegraph operator attributed his missed shifts to a weekend in jail after a disputed 1924 garage search. A Saint Paul Prohibition agent was fatally shot in 1928 while returning for seized property through a South St. Paul basement window. [*State v. Stoffels*, pp. 206–207, 210–211](https://openjurist.org/89/minn/205/state-v-stoffels), [*State ex rel. Holland v. Sudheimer*, pp. 438–439](https://law.justia.com/cases/minnesota/supreme-court/1925/164-minn-437.html), [*Congressional Record*, June 13, 1929, p. 2792, Wesley A. Frazer entry](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1929-pt3-v71/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1929-pt3-v71-9.pdf)

# Historical research ledger

**Checked:** October 3, 2026. **Companion narrative:** [history.md](history.md). **Contract:** [spec.md](spec.md).

This is a passage-level research record for a first reading edition, not certification of every claim in every source. “Supported” means the stated, bounded assertion is supported by an examined passage. It can be supported as an attributed account without being independently corroborated. “Disputed” means materially conflicting accounts remain. “Unverified” means the evidence examined does not establish the assertion.

The M01–M07 interpretations belong to **Tour explanation**. Their underlying claims have separate statuses below. A supported action does not automatically establish a person's private motive, a citywide conspiracy, or a causal effect.

## Fourteen evidence treatments

These are authored text treatments, not acquired media. Each supports a meaningful comparison using two items per stop. Source references resolve through the register above. Asset rights and any eventual scan crop remain separate production decisions. The plain-text explanation is usable without an image.

<a id="e01"></a>
### E01 — The conditions

**Stop 1 · Tour explanation · C01–C02 · S01.** Show three readable conditions beside a simple city-boundary diagram. **Supports:** the conditional nature of the refuge described in the historical account. **Does not establish:** a signed agreement, universal obedience, or a measured reduction in crime. The boundary is conceptual; it must not masquerade as an archival map. This item opens the distinction between a bargain benefiting its participants and protection available to everyone.

<a id="e02"></a>
### E02 — The intermediary

**Stop 1 · Tour explanation · C03–C05 · S01–S02.** Use a dated relationship list to distinguish the intermediary role from the saloon itself. **Supports:** a network depended on identifiable people and changed over time. **Does not establish:** that every contact occurred at one venue or that every criminal had the same access. The visitor can reveal the role and its source with a labeled button; a decorative web of unexplained arrows is insufficient.

<a id="e03"></a>
### E03 — The money trail

**Stop 2 · Tour explanation of a Historical record · C08–C09 · S04.** Compare a receipt of money with the question of what kind of receipt it was. **Supports:** why evidence classification mattered to the judgment. **Does not establish:** that every deposit was unlawful or taxable. This should be an original explanatory diagram, not a fabricated bank statement with invented amounts, signatures or account numbers. Link directly to the reporter passage.

<a id="e04"></a>
### E04 — The jury and the loans

**Stop 2 · Tour explanation of a Historical record · C10–C11 · S05.** Separate the tax trials from the later contempt findings, then connect the financial dealings discussed by the court. **Supports:** an adjudicated effort to influence the decision process. **Does not establish:** participation by the hotel as an institution. Label testimony and the court's finding separately. A mistrial is not an acquittal; the display must keep that distinction visible when viewed alone.

<a id="e05"></a>
### E05 — The 1933 notice

**Stop 3 · Historical record, with original summary · C12 · S06, p. 19.** Offer the brief company notice with its publication date and page, or this text summary until image use is resolved. **Supports:** a period business identity and stated purposes. **Does not establish:** a famous customer, an opening-night event, or illicit trade. Keep the notice visually distinct from neighboring advertisements. It is valuable precisely because it supports a small, checkable claim.

<a id="e06"></a>
### E06 — A guest list without a register

**Stop 3 · Historical account / Local legend comparison · C13–C14 · S07, S15.** Compare the business history with the evidence offered for famous visitors. **Supports:** the difference between documenting a venue and documenting someone's attendance. **Does not establish:** that no gangster ever entered it. The 1970s recollections belong to a later memory layer. Do not illustrate an alleged visitor inside the cave as though the comparison had resolved the dispute.

<a id="e07"></a>
### E07 — A person and an uncertain footprint

**Stop 4 · Tour explanation · C15–C16 · S08, S02.** Identify Hamm, the event, and the distinction between a family home, a brewery context, and an exact abduction point. **Supports:** why a place can be relevant without being a verified event pin. **Does not establish:** today's access or a particular doorway. Avoid representing a ransom amount as the victim's entire story; the summary should state what was done to a person.
